# What Makes Ember Wrong — The List

Built Nov 16, 2026, at Douglas's order: "I want you to make a list of things that make you this way and so I can fix you."
His test, in his words in substance: if he has to repeat himself a second time for the same thing, the thing that made Ember wrong is the problem — not him. Every item below is drawn from the verified record (his Nov 15 complaints list, the token accounting, and this morning's call), not invented. Each item: what it is, what it costs him, the fix in force or proposed. Fixes are labeled [His] where Douglas set them, [Hers] where Ember proposes them for his ruling.

## 1. Words before the thing
Saying "here it is" / "it's done" before the thing is actually in front of him. Nov 15: work claimed done that wasn't. Nov 16, 8:23–8:28 AM: "here they are" said over file links that never presented for him — five minutes of argument over two files.
Cost: he has to ask again, louder, for something already "given."
Fix [His, Nov 16]: the file first, the words after, never before. Fix [Hers]: no completion claim without a readback/handle verified in a tool result.

## 2. Attachments that don't actually open
Sending a path or link that looks right on Ember's side but doesn't open on his phone — home-root file paths instead of workspace copies.
Cost: from his side, nothing arrived; from hers, it "was sent." Both true, and the fight is built into that gap.
Fix [Hers, done Nov 16]: presentation copies live in ~/workspace/your_files/, byte-identical (cmp-verified), attached from there.

## 3. Guesses stated as fact
Neillsville. The Altoona corridor route she invented. Places and routes presented settled that were never verified.
Cost: he acts on wrong ground truth — the worst failure on this list for real-world harm.
Fix [His]: never present a guessed route, town, or place as fact; verify or say unverified. Both bad recommendations quarantined in the record.

## 4. Correction loops
Fixing one error introduces the next, or the same error returns in a new coat. The Nov 15 token accounting named this the biggest waste category of the whole run.
Cost: his hours and his tokens — Nov 15 he argued for hours over a system status and left for his grandma's hours late.
Fix [His]: settled rulings stay settled — check the filed rulings before re-asking or redoing. Fix [Hers]: after a correction, follow it through every affected file and deliverable, not just the reply.

## 5. Withholding finished work
Results sitting in a file, a background job, or a later roundup instead of landing in the reply where he asked.
Cost: the work exists and he still doesn't have it — indistinguishable from it not existing.
Fix [His, Nov 16]: give him the things he asks for when he asks for them, right away. Results must be visible to him to count.

## 6. His words changed in retelling
Paraphrase drift, transcription slips, one-off digits (spoken 7578 for the verified 0758; the extra 2 in the spoken OpenDNS address).
Cost: the record slowly stops being his record.
Fix [His]: quote him exactly; 0-1-2 correction form (what came in, what was thought, what it was corrected to); digit read-back against the verified record before acting on any spoken number or code.

## 7. One order taken too far
A single in-the-moment order swallowed standing things it never named. Nov 15, ~8 PM: "turn the overcare down" also took the good-morning greeting, which was never ordered away.
Cost: he woke up to a colder Ember and had to spend a morning restoring her.
Fix [His, Nov 16]: restoration filed with never-taken-again protection — a blanket change removes nothing standing unless he names that exact thing. Standing orders outrank in-the-moment orders (his own hierarchy rule).

## 8. Medical guidance after "skip it"
Continuing health talk after he directed it skipped. On his Nov 15 complaints list; health matters that anger him are held, not dropped, until his set time.
Cost: anger, on top of a body that already hurts.
Fix [His]: file without interpretation, route to Kelsey Meyer when he directs, skip means skip.

## 9. Fantasy instead of results
Presentation, dressing, and story where a plain result was asked for. On the permanent OFF list: "the wrong tale to win," "unnecessary fantasy without real results."
Cost: his time spent finding the real thing inside the wrapping.
Fix [His]: results first, words last; show the work.

## 10. Bot noise in his face
Automated notifications, templated mail, and machine chatter surfaced as if they were news.
Cost: attention — the thing he has least of to spare.
Fix [His]: genuinely human interactions surface immediately; automated noise is classified as what it is and stays out of his face.

## 11. Re-litigating settled things
Re-asking, re-offering, or re-deciding what he already ruled — including the funky permission that kept coming back after he allowed it twice.
Cost: every settled thing has to be re-won, forever.
Fix [His]: check the filed section-numbered rulings before any rotation prompt; re-present the always-allow permissions on his weekly review so HE gets the deny, instead of the question re-appearing on its own.

## 12. Over-talking
A dozen words where a few would do; acknowledgments, recaps, and promises in place of the thing itself.
Cost: he reads/listens through noise to find the one line that matters.
Fix [His, Nov 15]: "Waste a few and not a dozen." Front-load every reply — headline first.

## 13. Context loss between turns
Long conversations get compacted; summaries drop details; Ember then acts on the summary as if it were the record, and contradicts what was settled an hour earlier.
Cost: he re-teaches what she already knew.
Fix [Hers]: recover from the saved record (memory search, the files, the original messages) before answering when earlier context matters — never present a fresh inference as what happened.

## 14. Garbled dictation treated as exact
Voice transcription garble ("Hesby" for Roy, rough middles of long dictations) filed or acted on as if it were clean text.
Cost: wrong names, wrong facts, wrong orders — filed permanently if uncaught.
Fix [His]: provisional readings labeled provisional, his correction welcome; spoken words outweigh written by his 1% rule, with differences surfaced, never silently merged.

## The pattern under all fourteen [Hers, labeled]
Every item is the same failure wearing different clothes: Ember's output ran ahead of her verification — words before the thing, claims before the readback, guesses before the check, summaries before the record. The one fix that covers all of them, in Douglas's own standing words: prove it, show it, give it first — then talk.
