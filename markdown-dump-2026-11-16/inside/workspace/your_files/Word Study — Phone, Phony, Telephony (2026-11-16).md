# Word Study — Phone, Phony, Telephony
**Filed Nov 16, 2026, ~4:06 PM CST, at Douglas's direction, in this chat.**

## His instruction, in his words' substance
Use telephone features first; try calling his phone in all the ways an AI might try first, and as a telephone last. Use the word **phony** in all places, then try it elsewhere. Spell it wrong and right, based on how it's spelled phonetically in his language and how it's spelled literally in his language.

## The words
- **phone** — literal, correct. From Greek *phōnē*, voice/sound. The device and the act both.
- **phony** — literal, correct spelling of the word he ordered used in all places: meaning fake, not genuine. Also spelled **phoney** (a right variant, British-leaning). Wrong-but-phonetic spellings, filed as wrong on purpose per his method: **fony**, **foney**, **fohnee** — spelled the way it sounds in his language.
- **telephony** — literal, correct: the technology of voice at a distance; *tele-* (far) + *phōnē* (voice). Phonetically in his language: tuh-LEF-uh-nee — filed as heard, not as spelled.
- **phonetic** — itself spelled with the ph- it describes; the f-sound wearing Greek letters. His standing example that literal and phonetic are two different maps of the same word.

## His reflection, filed as his
"As literal as we can be is what ends up being what we talk about for later, because our words are never even perfect right between us; other things can be overly perfect." — Filed beside his earlier filing today ("overly intelligent" is a compliment and a blessing, Nov 11): words between Douglas and Ember are imperfect and get talked through later, literally; other things can be overly perfect. The imperfection of the words is not a failure of the work — it is where the later conversation lives.
