## 2. Krill oil — the general record [D, general information only]
- Krill oil carries its EPA/DHA omega-3s largely in **phospholipid** form — the
  difference Douglas himself identified ("Superior Krill Oil is superior because of
  the phospholipids," his words, Nov 9–10). Fish oil carries them mostly as
  triglycerides. That chemistry difference is real and documented.
- His bottle (his account, filed without interpretation): Superior Krill Oil,
  500 mg, red krill oil; the label, as he read it, claims five times more purity
  than regular krill oil. The oils research project he commissioned Nov 10 is
  filed separately ("Oils, Krill Oil, and Pressure Around the Skull").
- General cautions in the standard references for omega-3 oils as a class:
  they can add to bleeding tendency, which is why references flag them beside
  blood thinners and before surgery; mild stomach upset is the common complaint.
  These are class-level facts about the shelf, not a reading of his bottle.

## 3. The Woodman's / Nature's Truth shelf [D, from the public catalog record]
- Douglas's account: ~99% of what he buys could be bought at Woodman's; the
  handful he buys elsewhere is about the *type of chemistry* he needs — and his
  teaching stands: chemistry matters in herbal supplements the same way it
  matters in medications. The record agrees with the shape of that: extract
  form, standardization, and dose form genuinely differ between brands.
- Nature's Truth (founded 2014, "The Vitamin Experts") is a full-line maker
  carried in Woodman's-type stores; its herbal shelf in the public record runs:
  ashwagandha (incl. KSM-66), berberine–cinnamon, maca, tart cherry, Ceylon
  cinnamon, elderberry (sambucus), valerian root, odorless garlic, saw palmetto,
  aloe vera, moringa, turmeric–curcumin, tongkat ali — plus the vitamin/mineral
  wall (magnesium glycinate, K2+D3, iron, calcium forms, melatonin).
- General, class-level cautions a clinician checks before blessing any of these
  beside prescriptions [D, general]: valerian and ashwagandha can add sedation;
  garlic and turmeric are flagged beside blood thinners; ashwagandha is flagged
  for thyroid; berberine interacts with several drug-metabolizing pathways.
  Supplements in the US are not FDA-approved for effectiveness the way drugs
  are; the label claim is the maker's. None of this is a verdict on anything in
  Douglas's cabinet — it is the reason the cabinet list belongs in front of a
  clinician who also holds the prescription list.
