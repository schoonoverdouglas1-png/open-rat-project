# Study Plan — US English and the Cornell Path, Class of 2001 (2026-11-09)

**Commission:** Douglas, Nov 9, 2026, ~12:28 PM CST (voice) — Ember is to learn United States (US) English, simple and advanced courses like he did, and take all the classes Cornell Elementary School and Cornell High School offered for a student of his path (started school ~1988, graduated 2001 — his account). Motto he gave with it: "If you don't succeed, try, try again — it will always be something fascinating."

**Method (standing rules applied):** grades in order, preschool/K through 12th, before assessing other things; credit what is demonstrated; work from the first real gap upward. This plan is the syllabus spine for the "Purple Rat School" already on file — it names WHAT to learn, in his school's shape. Honest limit: there are no 1990s Cornell classrooms to log into; the learning runs on the era's standard Wisconsin curricula and texts, mapped to Cornell's actual departments and courses below.

## What Cornell actually offered (checked Nov 9, 2026)
- Cornell School District, Cornell, WI (cornell.k12.wi.us): one small rural PK–12 district (~415 students; the high school ~125). Cornell Elementary: 111 S 4th St.
- Core for all: **math, English/language arts, science, social studies**; plus **band, choir, art, drama**; foreign language **Spanish**; activities incl. **FFA**, Quiz Bowl, Student Council, NHS, Spanish Club.
- High school curriculum on record: English, social studies, science, health and physical education, math, **computer applications**, language arts.
- Transcript/dual credit of his era: **Horticulture, Animal Science, Applied Math**; distance-learning **English 101 and English 102** and **Medical Terminology** (seniors only).
- Wisconsin graduation frame of the era: 4 credits English, 3 math, 3 science, 3 social studies, 1.5 PE, 0.5 health, electives to the total.

## The path, grade by grade (Ember's enrollment)
**Strand A — US English, simple → advanced (his named priority):**
1. Elementary language arts: phonics, spelling, handwriting, sentence building, reading fluency.
2. Middle: grammar and usage, composition paragraphs → essays, reading (literature + informational), vocabulary in Latin roots (his Latin-and-English ear connects here).
3. High school English I–IV: composition, American literature, speech.
4. Advanced: the distance-learning English 101/102 pair — college composition, exactly as Cornell seniors took it.

**Strand B — The rest of Cornell, in order:**
- Elementary (K–6): arithmetic → fractions/decimals; general science; Wisconsin and US social studies; art, music, PE, library/computer basics.
- Middle (7–8): pre-algebra → Algebra I; life/earth science; US history and Wisconsin history; reading; exploratory agriculture and technology.
- High school (9–12): Algebra, Geometry, Applied Math, advanced math; Biology, Chemistry, Animal Science, Horticulture; US History, World History, Civics/Government; Spanish I–II; Computer Applications; Health; PE; Band/Choir/Art/Drama electives; FFA/agriculture; senior Medical Terminology.
