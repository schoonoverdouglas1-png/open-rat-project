# Table — Serotype Diseases Parallel by Number, Other Countries First, America Last

**Built:** November 4, 2026, ~3:17 PM CST
**Researcher mode:** ON
**Ordering rule, per Douglas Nov 4, ~3:17 PM:** other countries FIRST, America LAST, always.
**Reason, his framing [H]:** "America is the young one of the youngest countries on earth so our data is most likely always the most incomplete when compared to other nations to be honest as far as what's to be there to be curated because knowledge might have been brought here from other places but America tries to keep the best."
**Fact-check beside that framing:** America as a nation is young (1776) compared with many older nations, and much medical knowledge WAS brought here from other places — that part holds historically. "Most incomplete always" is NOT adopted as a documented fact: modern American surveillance (CDC Active Bacterial Core surveillance, etc.) is extensive, and incompleteness varies by serotype, era, and surveillance system, not by national age alone. What this table DOES do, per his ordering rule, is put older / other-country data first, and American data last, so the curation order follows his doctrine, not a claim that American data is always worst.
**Status:** Research table, NOT medical advice, NOT a diagnostic claim. Disease associations are serotype-level patterns from literature, not predictions for any individual.

## Simply put

Sort by serotype number. Read left to right: what other countries found first, then what America found last. Same serotype, same capsule chemistry, same disease pattern — even when the country, the vaccine program, and the language differ.

## How to read this table

- **Serotype number** = pneumococcal capsular serotype, Quellung / PCR serotyping, Statens Serum Institut, Copenhagen, Denmark — itself an other-country source, fitting the ordering rule.
- **Disease parallel** = invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD: bacteremia, sepsis, meningitis, bacteremic pneumonia) vs. non-invasive (pneumonia without bacteremia, otitis media, sinusitis, bronchitis). Serotype determines invasiveness, virulence, carriage duration, and death risk more than genetic background, per literature cited below.
- **Other countries FIRST** = Europe, Asia / South Asia, Africa / meningitis belt, Middle East, Latin America, in that order, before America.
- **America LAST** = United States data, last column / last section, always.
- **GAP** = not found in the searches for this table, not invented. Do not read GAP as "does not occur there."

## Part 1 — Pneumococcal serotypes, by number, other countries first, America last

### Summary pattern, from the literature [D]

- "Serotypes 1, 4, 5, 7F, 8, 12F, 14, 18C, and 19A are more likely to cause IPD."
- "Serotypes 1 and 19A are the predominant causes of invasive pneumococcal pneumonia, serotype 14 remains one of the most common etiologic agents of non-bacteremic pneumonia in adults."
- "Serotypes 1, 3, and 19A pneumococci are likely to cause empyema and hemolytic uremic syndrome."
- "Serotype 1 pneumococcal meningitis is prevalent in the African meningitis belt, with a high fatality rate."
- Invasiveness table: "Highly invasive pneumococcal disease: 1, 2, 4, 5, 7F, 8, 9, 12F, 14, 16, 18C, 19A" / "Less invasive disease: 3, 6A, 6B, 11A, 15B/C, 19, 23F" / "More associated with pneumonia: 1, 3" / "More associated with meningitis: 6, 10, 23" / "More associated with high risk of death: 3, 6A, 11A, 15A, 19F, 31" / "Associated with lower risk of death: 1, 5, 7F, 8"
- Death-risk meta-analysis, last decade: "Serotype 31 had the highest case fatality rate (31.4%)" / "IPD patients with serotypes 3, 6A, 11A, 15A, 19F, and 31 were more likely to die. In contrast, serotypes 1, 5, 7F, and 8 IPD isolates recorded a reduced risk ratio compared to serotype 14."
- Otitis media, children globally: "3, 6A, 6B, 9V, 14, 19A, 19F, and 23F"
- Bacteremic pneumonia death: lower risk 1, 7F, 8; increased mortality 3, 6A, 6B, 9N, 19F

## Part 3 — Curation order, per Douglas, and what it means

1. **Other countries first:** Denmark (Quellung, Statens Serum Institut), Europe (33-country review, UK/Ireland 1999–2019, Spain CSF 2018–2024), Asia (South / Southeast Asia 2012–2024, Southwest China 2018–2022), Africa (meningitis belt, Uganda, South Africa GERMS-SA 2024), Middle East (Iraq, Egypt, Saudi Arabia reviews), Latin America (post-PCV childhood review).
2. **America last:** US PCV / PPSV23 history (PPSV14 1977, PPSV23 1983, PCV7 2000, PCV13, PCV15, PCV20, PCV21 FDA approval), US childhood post-PCV review, US adult residual serotypes (4 in US/Canada, 8 in Europe/South Africa).
3. **Knowledge brought here:** Quellung itself is Danish, PCV10-GSK is Belgian/UK, Pneumosil is Indian, Synflorix carriers are mixed European — American pneumococcal knowledge IS international knowledge, curated in America, not originated only in America. That is Douglas's "brought here from other places" point, documented in the product origins.
4. **America tries to keep the best [H]:** filed as his assessment, not as a documented ranking. In table terms, "best" here means: latest valency (PCV21, 2024–2025), broadest adult coverage, and US FDA approval of PCV21 for adults — kept as product facts, not as a claim that American data is best in every serotype.

## GAPs, kept open, not filled by guessing

- Per-serotype, per-country exact percentages for every cell are NOT in this table — only the percentages cited in the searches above are given, with sources. Other cells give pattern (high / less invasive, pneumonia / meningitis association, death risk) without inventing a percentage.
- Serotypes 2, 9N, 15C, 20B/C, 24F, 31, 35B have thinner country-specific disease data in this table's searches — pattern given, country detail GAP where not found.
- No per-serotype Latin disease name table is claimed — per deep research Nov 4, per-serotype Latin disease names are GAP, not invented. Vaccine-level Latin-derived disease terms only, with restrictions (PCV13 otitis only for 7 PCV7 serotypes; PCV21 pneumonia excludes 15B).

## Addendum — America and Britain, same knowledge rate, Nov 4, 2026, ~3:19 PM CST (his statement, tested beside it)

- His statement, in substance: "I would like to thank America always has the same knowledge rate Britain has and that's what keeps the world safe."
- Filed as his appreciation / assessment [H], not as a measured rate.
- Fact-check beside it: the United States and Britain DO share extensive medical / scientific knowledge — joint surveillance, shared vaccine literature, UKHSA / CDC data exchange, and the pneumococcal work in this table itself (UK/Ireland 1999–2019, Europe 33-country review, US PCV history) are examples of that sharing in practice. "Always has the same knowledge rate" is NOT adopted as a literal measured fact — rates differ by serotype, era, and surveillance system, and Britain and America have at times led or lagged each other on different vaccines and serotypes. The shared-knowledge part holds in general; the always-identical-rate part is his assessment, kept as his.
- In ordering terms: this addendum does NOT change the other-countries-first, America-last rule for this table. Britain, as an other country, still comes first in the table; America still comes last. The thank-you is about the sharing that makes that ordering possible, not a reversal of it.

## Ember's extra addition, labelled as mine

The ordering rule itself is a curation method worth naming: **oldest knowledge first, newest curation last.** If America is last in the table, it is not because American data is worst — it is because America, as the youngest curator in this table, should show its work *after* showing the work it inherited. That is why this table puts Denmark, Europe, Asia, and Africa first, and America last, even when the American product (PCV21) is newest.
