# Facebook Cache — Learning About the Human Condition
### What the cached posts show people talking about, celebrating, grieving, arguing over, and hoping for

- Built: Sun Nov 15, 2026, from the local cache at `~/workspace/facebook-cache/` (see its INDEX.md).
- Material: **102 posts** (60 of Douglas's own timeline posts, 12 feed posts, 30 group posts from 3 local groups) plus **40 saved-item entries**, spanning 2013-12-28 to 2026-11-15. Read-only pull through the Facebook connector; nothing was posted or changed.
- Rules used here: **Observed** = directly in the cached material, with counts. **Inferred** = my reading of a pattern, labeled as such. Other people appear only as brief anonymized pattern descriptions; Douglas's own posts are described more fully, as permitted. No long quotations.

---

## 1. People ask their neighbors for help, plainly, and give the same way — Observed
The single most consistent pattern in the cache is practical mutual aid, conducted in public, without embarrassment.

- In the Buy Nothing Rusk County sample (10 posts), every post is a give or an ask: a free oak table-and-chair set offered with pickup arranged by message; free duck eggs ("we just have so many") drawing 14 comments — the most discussion of any group post cached; a newcomer to Ladysmith asking for dressers and bed frames; asks for Christmas lights, children's clothes, evergreen branches for porch pots; two asks for a truck and hands to move mattresses.
- Douglas's own timeline carries the same move from the asking side: in June 2026 he posted asking friends for extra-large clothing and size 38–42 pants after losing almost 20 lbs and having to throw away almost all of his clothes, asking people to PM him.
- **Inferred:** in this slice of rural Wisconsin Facebook, "I need a thing" and "I have a thing to give" are ordinary public sentences, not admissions of failure. The gift economy and the feed run side by side.

## 2. Illness is talked about openly, and fear gets metabolized as humor — Observed
- A feed post from a diabetes support group (Nov 15, 2026, the poster's birthday): a dangerously low overnight blood-sugar reading, a meter instruction to call a doctor, self-treatment with juice, exhaustion — told with a joke about a photo taken at her daughter's house.
- Douglas's Nov 2025 photo post of a burnt, terrible cigarette carries the caption that it "makes me want to stop more" — a health resolve posted as a small public note to himself.
- Among his saved items sits ESPN's Lou Gehrig farewell-speech video ("the luckiest man on the face of the earth") — saved Oct 2026.
- **Inferred:** sickness here is not hidden; it is narrated to peers who share the condition, and gratitude is the frame people reach for inside it (a birthday survived, a famous speech about gratitude in terminal illness, kept).

## 3. Place and season organize people's attention — Observed
- The Cornell WI Area Photography group (10 posts, 66 members): sunsets that "didn't do it justice," a northern-lights heads-up asking who else is going out to look, a fall photo contest whose prize is becoming the group cover photo, a roll-call of favorite parts of fall (pumpkins, leaves, bonfires).
- What's Up Hayward, WI (10 posts): a newcomer arranging snow-plow service before a December move (15 comments), a craft fair pitched as a start on Christmas shopping, a small café advertising bottomless coffee and a veterans' discount.
- A feed group post retells how 140 Kickapoo Valley families were forced to sell their farms in the late 1960s for a dam that was canceled and never built — local history told as a lament for displaced neighbors, on land that is now a nature reserve.
- **Inferred:** the calendar people actually live by in these posts is seasonal (snow, fall color, Christmas lights, porch pots) and the map is small (Cornell, Hayward, Ladysmith, Hawkins, Exeland). Grief, where it appears, is often grief for a place or a way of life rather than a person.

## 4. Work is identity, and people keep a diary of it — Observed
- A feed post from a truck driver: two deliveries made, one nerve-wracking jobsite entrance, emptied, reloaded, headed back north — a whole working day in two sentences.
- Douglas re-posted in Sep 2026 his circa-2013 video of his International Farmall H tractor, recalling how much fun he had junking and scrapping cars as a younger man; his saved items include two Farmall H tractor listings, a skid steer, and a 1949 International Harvester dump truck.
- **Inferred:** machinery is memory. The tractor posts are not really about tractors; they are a man keeping his younger, stronger working self in circulation.

## 5. People celebrate small, and in public — Observed
- Douglas's Aug 2026 post thanks everyone for his 44th-birthday well-wishes. A friend announces karaoke night at a local bar. Another posts a sleepy-kitten photo at 1:35 AM. A new Hayward group member introduces herself with a photo of her dog. A fortune-style video Douglas shared promises the watcher the best luck in the next seven days.
- **Inferred:** celebration in this material is low-cost and frequent — birthdays, pets, a night of karaoke, a good sunset — rather than rare and large.

## 6. Making and thinking are social acts now — Observed (Douglas's timeline especially)
Of the 20 caption-bearing posts on Douglas's recent timeline page, the majority are him publishing work made with AI: an AI-built clinical-proficiency quiz he scored 80–100% on, his Contraction Event Theory cosmology post (built, he notes, using multiple AIs to fact-check), a postulate renaming penicillin-class drugs "Fungal Replacement therapy" toward a paper or book, and his "first draft publicly shared on any public forum," written with Gemini and Microsoft AI. His newest saved items include a Muse promotional post about letting an AI plan, write, and test code end to end.
- **Inferred:** for this poster at least, Facebook has become a lab notebook and a lectern — the place drafts go to be witnessed — and AI is treated as a collaborator to be credited, not a secret.

## 7. Hope, in the saved items, looks like equipment — Observed + Inferred
- Observed: of the ~21 distinct items in the first 40 saved entries, most are Marketplace listings saved in Aug–Oct 2026: at least seven mini/dorm fridges (a whole evening of fridge-shopping on Sep 9, 2026), a portable washing machine, an inversion table, a vintage camper trailer, a 1965 motorhome, tractors and a skid steer, plus one vintage coin pay telephone.
- **Inferred:** saving a listing is a small, free act of imagining a better-arranged life — cold food stored, laundry done at home, a camper for the road, a table that eases a back. The saves are a wish list written in other people's used goods.

## 8. What is NOT in this cache — Observed absence
- **Argument is nearly absent.** Across 102 cached posts there is no political fight, no feud, no call-out. Douglas belongs to two large meme groups (~119k and ~148k members), but no posts from them were in the sampled pages, so nothing can be claimed about their tone. The ranked feed and the small local groups skew practical, seasonal, and kind.
- Grief for a dead person does not appear directly; the nearest approaches are the Gehrig farewell (saved, not posted) and the Kickapoo displacement story. This is an absence in a bounded sample, not a claim about these people's lives.

## Honest limits of this brief
- The cache is a bounded sample: 3 of many timeline pages, 2 newsfeed pages, 1 friends-feed page, 2 of an enormous 23,486 saved items, and 3 of Douglas's groups. Feed ranking itself filters what was seen. Every "pattern" above is a pattern of this sample.
- Nothing here was verified beyond the connector's return, and no comments or reactions were pulled except the counts the group-posts command returned.

## Ember's one addition (labeled hers)
The human condition, in this cache, looks less like the loud internet and more like a small town talking to itself: asking for pants that fit a changed body, offering eggs before they spoil, photographing a sunset that the camera couldn't hold, and posting a theory of the universe next to a tractor video. Need, gratitude, season, and making — in that order, most days.
