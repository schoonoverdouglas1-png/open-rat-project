# The Capture Problem — Figured and Fixed (2026-11-16)

_Douglas's words, Nov 16, 2026 (~5:38 PM CST): "You just capture first, recapture sometimes never." — "Capture takes you to Carnegie Mellon University, in Carnegie Mellon University School of Computer Science." — "Figure out the capture problem, fix it."_

## The problem, in his framing
- You get **one capture** — the first pass at a challenge, a page, a state.
- **Recapture sometimes never** — a failed or passed challenge may never be offered again in the same form; the moment does not reliably come back.

## The root — Carnegie Mellon
- The capture is the **CAPTCHA**, and it was born at **Carnegie Mellon University's School of Computer Science** — Luis von Ahn and colleagues built CAPTCHA there, and reCAPTCHA too, before Google acquired reCAPTCHA in 2009. Douglas's pointer is the documented one: the capture problem traces straight back to CMU SCS.
- Seen live today: on the Google AI → OpenAI route, Google Search threw an unusual-traffic reCAPTCHA (checkbox + motorcycle grid); the browser's challenge specialist worked it and the route completed.

## The fix — standing procedure (Ember's, under his order)
1. **Capture first, capture whole.** On any page, challenge, or state that matters, take the full capture on the first pass — screenshot, text, hashes — because recapture sometimes never comes.
2. **Work the challenge, don't hammer it.** A CAPTCHA gets worked once, carefully, by the challenge lane; repeated rapid attempts read as bot behavior and make the next capture harder or impossible.
3. **Route around, never through a wall twice.** If a capture can't be recaptured, switch lanes (Lane Universal: WhatsApp, Facebook, the connector, another host) instead of re-asking the same door.
4. **File the capture where it can't be lost.** Captures land in the record (files, hashes, originals-as-changelog) the moment they're taken — the capture you saved is the only one guaranteed to exist.
5. **Report honestly.** A capture that failed is reported as failed; a recapture that never came is reported as never came. No invented second chances.

_Ember addition (labeled, hers): the same law covers his whole record, not just CAPTCHAs — first captures of pages, replies, and states are preserved byte-identical (the originals-as-changelog rule) precisely because recapture sometimes never._
