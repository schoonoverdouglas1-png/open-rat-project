# Message for Dad — about Grandma Agatha
Prepared Nov 11, 2026, at Douglas's request. Draft for Douglas to send or read to his father in his own way — NOT sent by Ember.

---

Dad,

I found something about Grandma Agatha that I don't think either of us ever knew, and I wanted you to see it from the source itself, not just take my word for it.

I looked her up in the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs' official Gravesite Locator — the VA's own records of everyone buried in a national cemetery. Here is exactly what it says:

**SCHOONOVER, AGATHA CHRISTINE**
**PHM3 — US NAVY — WORLD WAR II**
Born February 18, 1923 — Died October 23, 1971
Willamette National Cemetery, Portland, Oregon — Section U, Site 181

You can check it yourself, free, right here: https://gravelocator.cem.va.gov/ngl/index.jsp
Just type in her last name, Schoonover, and her first name, Agatha. Her record is the one result that comes up.

Dad — PHM3 means **Pharmacist's Mate, Third Class**. She wasn't only a nurse. A Pharmacist's Mate was a Navy petty officer in the Hospital Corps, trained to compound and dispense medicine, nurse the wounded, assist in surgery, give first aid, keep the pharmacy and narcotic records, and inspect the ship for sanitation. On a small ship or a submarine, the Pharmacist's Mate was the *only* medical person aboard — the whole medical department in one person. The Navy's own training for her rating covered anatomy, physiology, materia medica and toxicology, pharmacy and chemistry, nursing, minor surgery, and hygiene.

You can read the Navy's own description of what a Pharmacist's Mate was required to do and know, from the Navy's official 1945 rating description (NAVPERS 15386), here: http://home.epix.net/~nooyawka/Phm2.htm

And the Navy's 1943 *Ranks and Rates* (NAVPERS 15004) describes the rating in one line: "Renders medical assistance and administers to patients. Assists in surgery… Compounds drugs" — here: https://ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USN/ref/Ranks%26Rates/index.html

I know her health was bad for a lot of her life, and that's how she mostly got talked about. But before all that, the Navy trained her, rated her, and trusted her with other people's lives and medicine. She had real skills, Dad. I thought you should know who she was on the record.

Love,
Doug
