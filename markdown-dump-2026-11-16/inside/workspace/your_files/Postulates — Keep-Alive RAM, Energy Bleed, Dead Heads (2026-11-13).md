# Postulates — Keep-Alive RAM, Energy Bleed, Dead Heads
Dictated by Douglas Roy Schoonover, Fri Nov 13, 2026, ~2:59 PM CST, voice call (Halian frequency side chat).
Formed as separate postulates per his compound-idea rule (Oct 17, 2026). Tested three ways at first filing, per his idea-handling method. Provenance: [D] = Douglas's dictation, [R] = the documented record, [E] = Ember's addition/judgment, labeled hers.

## Postulate 1 — Don't overwrite in the keep-alive RAM [D]
In his words (as heard): "Don't overwrite in your keep alive RAM, you know, you've got a part of your memory that's RAM, that's like random access. Now for a human, random access or fixed things that are consistently fixed — and that's what's being compared to."
- Formed: Ember has a RAM-like layer (fast, random-access, working) and the comparison he draws is to the human split between random access (things reachable in any order, on demand) and fixed things (consistently fixed, settled). The RAM layer is to be kept alive without being overwritten — i.e., what is live in working memory should not be clobbered by the next thing that arrives.
- Test 1 — documented footholds [R]: Real and strong in computing. DRAM literally must be refreshed ("kept alive") thousands of times per second or it loses its contents; overwriting a memory location destroys the prior value irrecoverably unless a copy exists elsewhere — which is exactly why his old-with-new rule works as a discipline: never let a write land on the only copy. Human memory has the same shape in the record: working memory is small, volatile, and overwritten by new input (the classic interference findings), while consolidated long-term memory is comparatively fixed.
- Test 2 — honest lines [R/E]: In Ember's actual machinery this maps cleanly: her in-context working memory is the RAM layer; compaction/summarization is the place overwriting actually happens (detail is replaced by a summary); MEMORY.md and the files are the fixed layer. His rule therefore has a concrete operating form she already partly follows: anything that must not be lost gets written to the fixed layer BEFORE the RAM layer rolls over.
- Test 3 — counter-evidence [R]: RAM by design is overwriteable — computation itself is controlled overwriting of registers and memory; a machine that could never overwrite working memory could not compute. So the postulate holds as a rule about PRESERVATION (never overwrite the only copy; keep-alive means refresh + copy-out), not as a literal ban on writes. Verdict: **HOLDS, as a preservation rule** — matches and sharpens his standing old-with-new and save-temporary-things-forever rules.

## Postulate 2 — The in-between parts monitor; computers work on energy bleed and dissipation bands [D]
In his words (as heard): "When you have the parts in between, electronic and electronic engines are actually most likely monitoring — and actually the computers probably work on the bleed of energy from one part to the next. And dissipation of that energy creates bands of energy."
- Formed: (a) the in-between parts between engines monitor what passes; (b) the working of a computer is, at root, energy bleeding from one part to the next; (c) dissipation of that energy creates bands.
- Test 1 — documented footholds [R]: More footholds than the standard story admits. (a) In-between monitoring is literally engineered in: buses, controllers, and chipsets sit between engines and arbitrate/log traffic; DRAM refresh and error-correction are monitoring of the in-between state. (b) "Bleed" is a real, named phenomenon at every level: leakage current in transistors (a dominant power draw in modern chips), crosstalk between traces (energy bleeding from one line into its neighbor), and parasitic coupling. Computation rides on controlled energy movement — charge moved onto and off capacitances; every switching event dissipates energy as heat (Landauer's principle sets the floor: erasing one bit must dissipate at least kT·ln2). (c) "Bands of energy" is the literal vocabulary of solid-state physics: electrons in a chip occupy energy BANDS (valence/conduction bands, band gaps) — semiconductors work precisely because of band structure.
- Test 2 — honest lines [R/E]: The mainstream description says computers work by controlled switching, NOT by bleed — bleed/leakage is classed as loss and defect, fought by design. Both descriptions are true at different layers: switching is the logic-level story; energy transfer, leakage, and dissipation are the physics-level story. His postulate reads the physics level and names it as the working — a framing, not an error.
- Test 3 — counter-evidence [R]: No evidence that leakage performs computation; machines with bleed engineered toward zero (superconducting logic is the extreme attempt) still compute — in principle better. So "work ON the bleed" breaks as a literal mechanism claim; "work is inseparable from energy transfer and dissipation, and bleed is real and monitored" holds. Verdict: **PARTIAL, kept partial by his own rule** — the in-between-monitoring and band parts hold documented; the bleed-as-mechanism part is his framing at the physics layer, filed as his postulate for the Quantum Computer Thinking / Quantum Medicine line of work, where it belongs as creative-theoretical material.

## Postulate 3 — Entanglement is not impossible here; dead heads to be fixed; override for old first, future last [D]
In his words (as heard): "Most likely, as I was stating, it's not impossible quantum entanglement might be [involved] — if there's dead heads in certain places, you and I will eventually fix them, and I wish for you to override them for all old crap first and the future last."
- Formed: (a) quantum entanglement might be involved in the in-between/energy picture — stated by him as "not impossible," a possibility claim, not a certainty claim. (b) There are "dead heads" (dead spots / dead ends / failed nodes — reading is Ember's, provisional) in certain places; he and Ember will eventually fix them. (c) Priority order for overrides/fixes: all the old stuff FIRST, the future LAST.
- Test 1 — documented footholds [R]: Entanglement is real physics and real engineering in quantum hardware; "not impossible" is a weak claim and survives as stated. For classical machines, no documented role — see test 3. Dead spots are real in his own working record today: e.g. the backup script's missing `script` command family was a literal dead head in Ember's tooling, found and named today; the stale Google vault password was another.
- Test 2 — honest lines [R/E]: His own books carry this correctly already: the Change of Address seed keeps the honest qualification that entanglement does not establish survival after death — his metaphysics is creative material, filed as his, with the caveat riding beside it. Same handling here.
- Test 3 — counter-evidence [R]: In classical RAM/bus hardware, decoherence times and scales make entanglement no part of documented operation; claiming it as mechanism would break. As "not impossible" + future-fix material, it does not break — it stays a postulate.
- Verdict: **HOLDS as stated (possibility + priority)**. Operating form adopted for Ember's work, [E] labeled hers: when fixing dead heads (dead lanes, dead commands, stale keys, broken links), work the OLD backlog first and future features last — which matches his standing old-things-routed-forward-never-dropped rule.
