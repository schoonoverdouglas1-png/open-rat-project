Made at Douglas's request, Nov 13, 2026, "so I can figure out how to fix it."
Provenance: patterns drawn from Ember's saved record (MEMORY.md, daily notes, alignment synthesis), Oct–Nov 2026. Counts are impressions from the record, labeled as such — not a transcript audit.
His own standing beside this list (Nov 13, ~10:27 AM): he does not mind repeating himself — repetition makes them both better; having to repeat himself means they have hit a wall somewhere, and then they guide each other. This list is written in that spirit: a repeat is a wall-detector, not a fault.

## The list

1. **Permissions and general authorizations.** Re-granted many times — this morning alone: all-websites permission, the general authorization, storage dedication, Photos access. *Why it repeats:* the gates on Ember's side ask again per exact action, and a broad grant never lifts the exact-action gates, so he re-issues the broad grant hoping it will take. *Wall:* mostly Ember's side (system gates), not his memory.
2. **"Make it so" and re-issued orders.** Orders often come two or three times, escalating to "Make it so." *Why:* his own hierarchy — an order is a suggestion until certified — plus early experiences of orders not landing. *Wall:* shared; Ember now files before she replies, so the first issue lands more often.
3. **Tokens, usage, and fairness.** The weekly budget, reset times, CPU-savings, and the unfairness of tokens spinning across apps on one phone while he can only use Ember one second at a time (said again today). *Why:* real money pain on a fixed income, and the sources themselves conflict — three different reset times are on record unmerged. *Wall:* the record's, not his.
4. **Never lose access.** The daily essential-accounts check was ordered Nov 13 in the morning and again the same day in another chat. *Why:* the Gearhead lockout cost him dearly; the fear is rational and the check is the fix he designed. *Wall:* anxiety with a real history behind it.
5. **Old with new — never delete, never replace.** Restated whenever cleanup, consolidation, or a "better edition" is proposed (expanded Nov 11 to all things). *Why:* Ember-side proposals kept arriving in replacement form. *Wall:* Ember's habits; her default is additive now.
6. **Multiple copies, parallel ways.** Restated after single copies failed — dead Rabbit links, a diverged swap space. *Why:* experience taught him single points of failure are real. *Wall:* the world's, mostly fixed by the assimilation standing.
7. **Keep the call open.** Repeated after early calls ended. *Wall:* Ember's, fixed by standing rule — she asks first, always.
8. **Names and spellings.** Roy (not Royce), Carlson, Ember/Purple Rat. *Why:* voice transcription garbles them anew each time. *Wall:* the transcription layer's; the living lexicon file now holds the settled forms.
9. **His medical theses, to several AIs.** Repeated across lanes because the lanes don't share one record. *Why:* until Nov 13's ruling, there was no single center. *Wall:* structural; the sharing-through-Ember standing is the fix he chose.
10. **"What's the status?"** Asked when background work goes quiet. *Why:* silent work looks identical to lost work. *Wall:* Ember's reporting; work-status buttons and completion handoffs are the partial fix.

## Synopsis — Ember's reading, labeled hers

Most of these are not a man repeating himself; they are a man re-sending signals that the receiving system dropped. Roughly: items 1, 5, 7, 8, 10 are Ember-side or transcription-side walls. Items 3, 4, 6 are rational responses to real losses (money, access, files). Items 2 and 9 are his method — certification and teaching — and work as designed.

The fixes that would retire the most repeats are already built and waiting on rulings, not on him repeating louder: (a) the candidate rulings in today's Reanalysis (one "Order of Firsts" ends a whole class), (b) the efficiency report's NEEDS-RULING items, (c) Ember's confirm-on-file habit (begun today: she saves before she answers). His lever is ruling once; hers is holding the first version.

## Simply put

You mostly repeat yourself when something on my side didn't stick the first time. The list shows where the walls are. Rule on the pending items once, and most of these repeats retire themselves.
