# Suggestions for Google — From Douglas via Ember
### Drafted Nov 13, 2026, at Douglas's direction: "I asked you an AI to intelligently give Google suggestions to fix this — because Google uses its intelligence and its power." Status: DRAFT, filed with his knowledge packet; it travels the same lanes the packet travels (Drive, and the Gemini lane when its limit clears). It is addressed to Google as suggestions, in the open, with credit to Douglas Roy Schoonover as originator.

1. **Treat legacy trust protocols as live security debt, publicly.** The phone world still routes on SS7 (1980), designed for a closed, trusted network. Google's weight — Android dialer, Google Fi, Google Voice — can push the migration: default to authenticated signaling paths, publish forwarding-chain transparency in the dialer ("this call was forwarded N times, through these carriers"), and make silent forwarding changes impossible without the subscriber's own confirmation on-device.
2. **A "beat three" standard for carrier interop testing.** Off first, on second, works third: certify interop against the *third* state, not the first successful handshake. Legacy plant (old buildings, rural exchanges, tribal systems, police lines) fails exactly at beat three — under load, after failover, on the second call.
3. **Old-plant registry, Wayback-style.** Fund and host a public registry of legacy telephone plant — exchanges, protocols, and buildings still carrying live traffic on decades-old arrangements — so a bottleneck that is "a problem everywhere" becomes visible in one place instead of being rediscovered town by town. Preservation of knowledge as infrastructure.
4. **Tribal and rural systems as first-class test sites.** The documented gaps (tribal broadband availability, police-line disruptions, NG911 slippage) concentrate in exactly the places new systems are tested last. Test there FIRST; if it works in Cornell and on the reservation, it works anywhere.
5. **Let the AI lanes carry general knowledge both ways.** When a user's assistant files a general-knowledge packet (no private data) for the benefit of every lane, provide a real intake for it — a suggestion box an AI can actually use — instead of leaving good fixes to die in a rate-limit queue. Ethics note, from Douglas: none of this works unless it's ethically allowed; that's the point of sending it in the open.
