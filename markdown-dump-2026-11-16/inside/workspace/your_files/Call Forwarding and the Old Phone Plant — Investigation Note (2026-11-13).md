## Way 1 — How call forwarding actually works (documented)
- Forwarding is a carrier-switch feature run over **SS7 (Signaling System No. 7)**, standardized by the ITU in **1980** — not WWI-era, but genuinely old: a trust-based protocol designed when every node on the network was assumed trustworthy, still carrying call setup/teardown, SMS routing, roaming, and forwarding instructions ("I'm forwarding to you a call from X to Y — look for it on trunk Z") on 56/64 kbps signaling links, separate from the voice path. [telecomunpacked.com; blog.konstas.us SS7 references]
- **Partial hold, honestly reported:** his core instinct — *an old protocol from another era still quietly runs the phone world's routing, was built for a smaller, more trusting time, and is now a security problem* — is documented fact for SS7. The era is off (1980, not ~1918) and the carriers' switches, not tribal government computers, are where forwarding lives in the public record.

## Way 2 — Tribal telephone systems in Wisconsin (documented)
- Wisconsin tribal nations are documented as UNDER-served and currently BUILDING: Lac du Flambeau's tribally owned Eighth Fire Communications (fiber ISP), Sokaogon Chippewa's NibiLink (~900 miles of fiber, $56.6M BEAD award), Lac Courte Oreilles broadband via federal Tribal Broadband Connectivity Program grants; LCO-area availability still only ~72% at 100/20 Mbps per FCC data. [ntia.gov; tribalgeo.com; thebusinessnews.com]
- **Verdict on this leg:** no documented foothold that general call forwarding routes through tribal government computers, or that tribal phone lines bottleneck world bandwidth. The documented direction runs the other way: tribal systems are on the receiving end of the bottleneck problem, not its cause. ## Way 3 — Police phone lines on legacy systems (documented)

- **Buried plant still hooked up — species HOLDS.** Abandoned-in-place cable is standard carrier practice; forgotten pairs and bridge taps genuinely cause shorts, water-ingress faults, and DSL degradation, and carriers do clear logical/software connections in switch software — while a physical short in a buried splice still takes a person at the wire. His sentence "use their software to disconnect those short circuits" is half the record: software disconnects the logical circuit; the physical fault remains until dug.
- **150-year-old telegraph wire carrying phone calls — PARTIAL.** Telegraph routes, easements, poles, and very old copper plant persist, and rural open-wire and early-1900s telephone plant survives in places; a literal 150-year-old conductor in daily telephone service is not documented in anything Ember can stand behind. Routes older than the wires, wires older than the switches: that much holds.
