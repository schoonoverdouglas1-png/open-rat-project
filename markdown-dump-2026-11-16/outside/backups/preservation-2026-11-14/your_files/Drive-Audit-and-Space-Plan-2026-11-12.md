## The space plan — saving space WITHOUT deleting anything

**Honest rule first (Ember's):** a compressed copy *added beside* its originals saves nothing — it uses more. Compression only saves space when the zip *replaces* the loose files, and replacing is a form of removal, so that step waits for your exact word, file list in front of you. What can be done freely now is organization and preparation:

1. **Organize by folder (moves only, no space change, needs your word per the sharing-card rule):** group the 88 top-level items into the pattern you already started — Books & Manuscripts, Research Bundles, Persona & Crew Files (beside the Gemini Pack), Medical Research, Resumes, Artifacts & Media. Same files, same total size, far easier to find.
2. **Compression candidates, if you later say the word (estimates):**
   - All loose text — Markdown (~602 KB), plain text (~573 KB), HTML (~240 KB) — zips down roughly 70–80%: ~1.4 MB → ~0.3–0.4 MB, **saving ≈ 1.0 MB**.
   - The pillowcase HTML artifact (227 KB, untouched since August) is inside that same text sweep.
   - **Not worth compressing:** the 3.12 MB .docx and the 1.72 MB bundle are already compressed formats (a .docx *is* a zip); re-zipping them saves approximately nothing.
   - Realistic total Drive saving from compression: **~1 MB out of ~6.9 MB** — and ~1 MB out of the 14.6 GB actually used across your account. This confirms the headline: Drive is the wrong battlefield for space.
3. **Duplicates:** leave every copy in place, old-with-new. Their combined excess is ~49 KB.
4. **The real audit (recommended next, read-only):** a Gmail audit (largest messages/attachments, old mail volume) and a Photos storage check. That is where ~14.6 GB lives, and any plan to get under the limit has to be built there — still without deleting anything until you name what goes.
