## 2. The honest fact every platform section repeats

**[S, consistent across all comparisons] No tip platform meaningfully brings strangers to an unknown page by itself.**

- Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Liberapay, PayPal links: **bring-your-own-traffic.** Discovery on-platform is negligible.
- Patreon: has browsing/categories, but discovery for a brand-new page is weak; it rewards an audience you already bring [S].
- GitHub Sponsors: **no discovery at all** — "GitHub does not show your Sponsors profile to people who don't know you exist" [S, and consistent with GitHub's model]; the README sponsor button is the funnel.
- GoFundMe: the **one partial exception** — search, categories, and trending can surface a compelling, specific, truthful story to strangers [S]. That is a reason it works for emergencies, and a reason it is a poor fit for ongoing research support.
- Gumroad Discover: real marketplace discovery, at a steep price — **30%** on Discover sales vs 10% + $0.50 on direct sales [S].

**[R] So the plan is two jobs, not one:** (1) one clean donation door (Ko-fi, then Sponsors), and (2) a stranger-discovery engine that points at that door (§6). A page with no engine earns nothing, on any platform, at any fee.

## 3. Platform by platform

*Worked example used below: what Douglas keeps from one $10 gift, before any benefits/tax question — fees only.*

### 3.1 Ko-fi — RECOMMENDED FIRST

- **Fees [V, official]:** Free plan, *Free mode* (Contributor/Standard OFF): **0% on one-time tips, 0% on crowdfunding Goals**; **5%** on monthly tips, memberships, shop sales, commissions. *Standard/Contributor mode ON:* **5% on everything including one-time tips**, in exchange for supporter-only-content/branding perks. Processor fee on top everywhere: ~3% + $0.30 (Ko-fi help wording; Stripe US standard is 2.9% + $0.30).
  - **Discrepancy note [V]:** Third-party roundups conflict — some say "5% on all free-plan payments," some say "0% on tips." Ko-fi's own Help Center resolves it: the 0%-tips figure is real **only in Free mode with Contributor status off**; the toggle is in **Settings → Payment**. **Operational rule: check that toggle before sharing the link.** If it is on, a $10 tip loses an extra $0.50 for no needed benefit at this stage.
- **$10 example:** Free mode tip → Ko-fi $0, processor ~$0.59 → **keeps ~$9.41** [S arithmetic].
- **Payout / minimum [V/S]:** No Ko-fi minimum, no waiting period — paid directly and instantly into the connected Stripe or PayPal account. Stripe then pays the bank on its normal rolling schedule.
- **Individual eligibility [V]:** Yes — individuals, no nonprofit, no business required. Requires Douglas's own PayPal or Stripe account to receive anything.
- **Stranger discovery:** Bring-your-own-traffic. Has a public page, posts, gallery/shop, and Goals with progress bars — good *receiving* furniture, weak *finding* furniture.
- **Fit for the two fields:** Tips + two named Goals + (later) Shop/pay-what-you-want excerpts + commissions, all on one page. This is the only low-fee platform that holds both fields without forcing a membership business.
- **Only Douglas can do:** create the account; connect Stripe or PayPal (legal name, bank, ID/tax verification inside Stripe/PayPal); set the Contributor toggle decision; press publish.
- **Ember can prepare:** full page copy (§9 drafts), Goal texts for each field, FAQ, README/link placements, share-post drafts — all for his review, none posted by Ember unattended.

### 3.2 Buy Me a Coffee — simplest runner-up

- **Fees [S]:** **5% platform fee** on tips/memberships, plus Stripe processing (~2.9% + $0.30), plus small reported extras on subscriptions/payouts (~0.5% each) in some breakdowns — verify on its fee page at signup.
- **$10 example:** platform $0.50 + processing ~$0.59 → **keeps ~$8.91**.
- **Payout [S]:** Instant via Stripe Connect; no monthly fee.
- **Eligibility:** Individuals welcome; Stripe account required.
- **Discovery:** Bring-your-own-traffic; cleanest, simplest page of the three tip platforms.
- **[R] Verdict:** Perfectly good door, strictly worse math than Ko-fi Free-mode tips, and less furniture (no equivalent Goal/shop combination as flexible). Keep as the fallback if Ko-fi signup hits a wall — do not run both tip pages at once; split doors confuse strangers.

### 3.3 Patreon — later, only if memberships become real

- **Fees [V/S]:** **10% of income for creator pages published after August 4, 2025**, plus payment processing (~2.9% + $0.30 per pledge), currency/payout fees where they apply. Legacy 5%/8%/12% plans do not apply to a new page.
- **$10 example:** at the $1,000/month worked example in the sources, a new creator keeps roughly **$8.11 of each $10** once processing on small pledges is counted [S].
- **Payout [S]:** Monthly cycle; new-creator holds reported in the first 30–60 days.
- **Eligibility:** Individuals welcome.
- **Discovery:** Best membership tooling and the strongest (still modest) built-in discovery of the three — justified, in the comparisons, mainly once a creator is past roughly $1,000/month [S].
- **[R] Verdict:** Wrong first door for Douglas: highest mainstream fee, built for promised recurring content/perks — and promising perks in exchange for payments changes the tax character of the money (see §8). Revisit only if readers are actively asking for a membership.

### 3.4 GoFundMe — known to strangers, wrong shape for ongoing research

- **Fees [V, official help center]:** Free to create/manage; **no platform fee** for US personal fundraisers; **transaction fee 2.9% + $0.30 per donation** deducted automatically. GoFundMe's own example: a $50 donation leaves **$48.25**. Recurring donations carry an additional **5%** per donation [V]. Donors are offered an *optional* tip to GoFundMe, settable to zero — that tip is the donor's money to GoFundMe, not a cut of Douglas's funds [V/S].
- **$10 example (one-time):** keeps **$9.41**.
- **Eligibility [S]:** Adults (18+) in supported countries including the US; identity/bank verification required before payout; fundraiser claims must be truthful and lawful.
- **Discovery:** The strongest stranger discovery in this list — search, categories, shares, and local/news pickup — **but** it rewards a single, specific, urgent need with a clear spend, not "support my ongoing research."
- **Tax note for donors [S]:** Donations to a personal fundraiser for a specific individual are **not tax-deductible** charitable contributions; deductibility requires a qualified 501(c)(3).
- **[FLAG] Benefits note:** Wisconsin policy sources name GoFundMe/Kickstarter-type crowdfunding *by name* (§8). Any GoFundMe must come **after** the caseworker check, with timing/withdrawal planned in advance.
- **[R] Verdict:** Hold in reserve for one concrete, honestly-described need (example shape only: a specific piece of equipment or a specific printing/research cost), if Douglas ever chooses it — never as the general support door, and never with any claim that a manuscript treats, cures, or advises on any condition.

### 3.5 Liberapay — the purist's recurring lane

- **Fees [V, official FAQ]:** **Liberapay takes no cut** — it is a nonprofit funded by donations to itself. Only processor fees apply; Liberapay reports recent averages of ~3% (Stripe) and ~5.1% (PayPal).
- **Payout [V]:** Money goes immediately to the recipient's Stripe or PayPal account; Stripe then auto-pays the bank, PayPal holds a balance until spent/withdrawn.
- **Shape [V]:** **Recurring weekly donations only, in spirit** — one-time gifts are "not properly supported yet" (a donor can start and immediately stop). Donations are **capped at $100/week per donor per recipient** by design. **No perks, no rewards, no contracts** — transactions must not be linked to a promise of recompense.
- **Privacy [V/S]:** Donors can give without the recipient knowing exactly who gave; totals given/received are public by default with opt-outs. Profiles are verified by connecting controlled social/GitHub accounts — which fits Douglas's existing GitHub identity well.
- **Eligibility:** Anyone funding work through recurrent donations [V FAQ]; Stripe/PayPal account required.
- **Discovery:** Essentially none — bring your own audience; small, open-source-flavored user base.
- **[R] Verdict:** The best ideological match for open research (no cut, no perks, GitHub-linked), and the worst discovery. Add it third, as the "steady patron" lane linked from GitHub READMEs, once Ko-fi is producing the first strangers.

### 3.6 Open Collective — powerful, and too much machine for one person right now

- **Fees [S]:** The platform itself does not charge Collectives a subscription; money is held by a **fiscal host**, and the host takes its fee. The main open-source host, Open Source Collective (OSC), lists a **10% host fee on incoming funds**, no setup/minimum/recurring fee; processor fees additional. Other hosts vary, some above 10%.
- **Eligibility [S]:** Made for *projects/groups*, not lone personal support. OSC requires an active/meaningfully used open-source project, an accepted open-source license, organizational (not purely personal) repository ownership, and an authorized representative; review can take days and can decline poor fits. Other hosts set their own rules. An "independent collective" must supply its own legal entity/bank/tax handling.
- **Payout [S]:** Not a tip jar: funds are spent by submitting **expenses/invoices with receipts**, approved by the Collective admin then the host, paid typically twice weekly via PayPal or Wise.
- **Discovery:** Public transparent budget page; no meaningful stranger discovery.
- **[R] Verdict:** Real transparency credibility for Field 2 if a *project* (not Douglas personally) grows collaborators and grant money that needs a fiscal host. Heavy, slow, receipt-based, and its 10% host fee buys administration Douglas does not yet need. **Filed as a later option, not a starting door.**

### 3.7 GitHub Sponsors — second door, built for Field 2 (and the research repos)

- **Fees [V, GitHub Docs]:** **GitHub charges no fee on sponsorships from personal accounts — 100% goes to the sponsored developer.** Sponsorships from *organization* accounts carry up to **6%** (3% card processing + 3% GitHub service fee; invoiced billing can remove the card part).
- **Eligibility [V/S]:** Anyone who contributes to an open-source project and lives in a supported region (the US is supported). "Contributes" is broad in GitHub's own guidance: code, **documentation**, bug reports, triage, design, mentorship, project management, leadership. No star/follower minimum. Requires: sponsored-developer profile + tiers, **bank and tax information (W-9 for US)**, and **two-factor authentication enabled**.
  - *Practical tie-in:* the GitHub notice Douglas already has — 2FA required for his account by **December 21, 2026** — is the same switch Sponsors needs. One chore, two purposes. [From Douglas's own mail, Nov 7; filed, not created here.]
- **Payout [S]:** Via Stripe Connect. Reported timing: **first payout ~60 days after the first sponsorship**, then monthly (payouts around the 22nd for Stripe Connect; ACH/wire on a $100 threshold cadence in some accounts). Slow — this is a credibility lane, not quick cash.
- **Discovery [S]:** **None built in.** The funnel is the **Sponsor button / FUNDING.yml in each repository**, plus promotion elsewhere. Sponsors pay by card or Patreon (PayPal not supported for Sponsors since Feb 2023) [S].
- **Tax [S]:** GitHub does not withhold; sponsorships are generally not tax-deductible for sponsors, and the recipient remains responsible for their own tax position; tax forms may be issued via Stripe (see §8).
- **Fit:** Douglas's manuscripts and AI-collaboration work **already live in public repos** (`medical-research-and-continuous-updates`, `the-mycelial-transposition`, `quantum-medicine`, `muse-swap-space`, and the rest). Strangers in open-research/AI circles judge the work where it sits — the Sponsor button meets them exactly there.
- **Only Douglas can do:** apply in the Sponsors dashboard, submit bank + W-9, enable 2FA, accept the Sponsors terms.
- **Ember can prepare:** Sponsors profile copy, tier texts (support-only, no promised deliverables at first — see §8), and a `FUNDING.yml` draft pointing at Sponsors + Ko-fi, for his review before anything is committed.

### 3.8 PayPal donation links / PayPal.Me — a rail, not a door

- **Fees [S, from PayPal's published US schedules as summarized Nov 2026]:** Standard domestic *donation* transactions (Donate Button / donation checkout): **2.89% + $0.49**. Confirmed-charity rate 1.99% + $0.49 does **not** apply to an individual. Ordinary PayPal.Me person-to-person sends are priced under PayPal's sending/receiving rules, which differ by funding source and transaction type — verify the live fee page before quoting a number to any supporter.
- **Eligibility/caution [S]:** PayPal.Me is open to personal accounts, but multiple creator guides warn that (a) PayPal's *donation product* language is built for charities, (b) a personal account taking regular "donations" can draw holds/freezes, and (c) calling a payment a donation does not make it a tax-deductible charitable gift. Safer wording for an individual: **"tip" or "contribution," never "tax-deductible donation."**
- **Payout:** To the PayPal balance; bank withdrawal on PayPal's terms. Chargeback/dispute risk sits with the recipient on direct links [S].
- **Discovery:** None. A link only.
- **[R] Verdict:** Useful *inside* Ko-fi (as its PayPal payout rail) and as a bare link for someone who asks "PayPal?" directly. Not a public front door, and never labeled in a way that implies nonprofit status Douglas does not have.

### 3.9 Facebook fundraisers — CLOSED for personal causes

- **[V/S] Meta has discontinued personal fundraisers on Facebook.** What remains is nonprofit fundraising: a registered 501(c)(3), a nonprofit Page, Meta fundraising-tools approval; donations route through PayPal Giving Fund (1.99% + $0.49 in the US via the direct route), payouts in ~15–45 days for enrolled nonprofits (up to ~90 days unenrolled), and native recurring giving was discontinued in October 2023.
- **[R] Verdict:** **Not available to Douglas as an individual — do not plan around it.** Facebook's role in this plan is *discovery only*: public posts on his own profile/groups pointing strangers to the Ko-fi link (§6). That use is just ordinary posting, with his own hands/tap on any share, per standing rules.

### 3.10 Pay-what-you-want (manuscript excerpts / digital downloads) — the bridge between "donation" and "product"

- **Gumroad [S]:** Free to start; **10% + $0.50 per direct sale**; **30% on sales through Gumroad Discover**; Gumroad acts as merchant of record and handles sales tax/VAT. Pay-what-you-want is a product setting ($0+ minimum possible).
- **itch.io [S]:** Pay-what-you-want by default (your price is the floor); you choose itch's revenue share — default 10%, **can be set to 0%** — plus processing. Real browsing discovery in books/zines niches, smaller than Gumroad.
- **Ko-fi Shop [V]:** Already in §3.1 — 5% on shop sales in free modes, $0+ pricing possible, no second account needed.
- **Character warning [see §8]:** The moment a payment buys a *thing* — even pay-what-you-want — it stops looking like a pure gift and starts looking like a **sale** for tax purposes. That is not a reason to avoid it; it is a reason to keep clean records and keep the two lanes labeled: *tips = support, shop = purchases.*
- **[R] Verdict:** Phase 2, on Ko-fi Shop first (no new account): one short, honestly-described excerpt/reader per field at $0+ pay-what-you-want. It gives a stranger who will not "donate" a thing to *get*, and gives the discovery engine (§6) something concrete to link.

## 6. Stranger discovery — the engine (where donations actually come from)

Ranked by fit for a tiny current audience, both fields, zero spend:

1. **GitHub first (Field 2 + research credibility) [R]:** Sponsor/FUNDING button + one honest "Support this work" section in the README of each public research repo; a pinned profile README saying what the work is, in plain words, with the Ko-fi + Sponsors links. Strangers who land on a repo from any search are the warmest strangers there are — they arrived *for the work*.
2. **Searchable public writing:** Short, plain-language public posts drawn from already-public research notes (never personal/lab material), each ending with one support link. Topics strangers actually search: pneumococcal serotype groupings explained simply, "what is a serogroup," AI-collaboration methods, how two AIs check each other's work. One post a week beats ten in a day.
3. **Facebook, as reach only:** Public posts on Douglas's own profile (his hobby/music presence already lives there) and relevant public groups *where group rules allow support links* — read rules first; a removed post helps no one. The link points out to Ko-fi; Facebook itself collects nothing (§3.9).
4. **Ko-fi's own furniture:** Goals with visible progress, regular free posts on the page, and the Shop excerpt — Ko-fi pages are indexable and shareable; supporters found elsewhere can follow there and get updates without social media.
5. **Communities of the work, not of fundraising:** Open-source / AI-research spaces (GitHub discussions, relevant subreddits/forums, LessWrong-style long-form for the AI-safety-adjacent pieces — see §7) and independent-scholar/writing communities. Rule everywhere: contribute the work first, link second, never spam a support link into a thread that didn't ask.
6. **The Rabbit, as crew [per standing pattern]:** Meta AI can draft variations, suggest angles, and comment (her comments get fact-checked by Ember before use — standing rule). She cannot post for Douglas, hold money, or verify links; her A123-style placeholder links remain unverified until checked.
7. **What NOT to do [R]:** No buying followers/ads (no money, and it poisons trust), no donation asks inside medical-advice framing, no mass-messaging strangers, no posting the link in his clinicians' directions, no implying nonprofit/501(c)(3) status, no "tax-deductible" wording anywhere.

**Expectation, stated plainly [R]:** Stranger support for niche open research starts at *small and slow* — the first gifts usually follow a specific post or repo that traveled, not the page launch itself. The plan's job is to make sure that when a piece of work does travel, a clean, honest door is one click away, everywhere the work already lives.

## 7. Grants & fellowships — free to apply, no affiliation required (leads, verify each cycle)

*Deadlines and cycles change; every entry below is a lead to re-verify on the funder's own page before any work is spent. Ember can draft; Douglas submits (§5). None of these is income to plan around — they are lottery tickets with honest odds.*

**Small, rolling, individual-friendly (best first targets):**
- **The Pollination Project — seed grants up to $1,000, awarded daily [S].** For individuals/groups *without paid staff* doing social-change work benefiting community/planet; successful grantees can become eligible for up to $5,000 more. Rolling, short application, no nonprofit required. Best fit shape: a *specific community-benefit project slice* of the work (e.g., a public plain-language research-literacy project), not "fund my books" generally.
- **Awesome Foundation — $1,000 per chapter, usually monthly [S].** Autonomous local chapters fund "awesome" projects; anyone can apply online with a short form; no 501(c)(3) required. Fit: a concrete, delightful, finishable project (a public exhibit/reading, a printed community edition) beats an open-ended research ask.
- **PEN America — U.S. Writers Aid Initiative [S via Wisconsin Arts Board listing]:** one-time grants for US professional writers (fiction/nonfiction authors, poets, playwrights, translators, journalists) facing an *acute, short-term financial emergency*. Not project funding — filed so it exists on the record if an emergency ever makes it relevant.

**Established, competitive, no affiliation required (long shots, real money):**
- **NEH Fellowships [S]:** $5,000/month for 6–12 months of humanities research/writing resulting in a book/articles/editions; **independent scholars explicitly encouraged, no university affiliation needed**; applied as an individual via Grants.gov. Caveat: the 2026 cycle's stated focus was American history/culture and Western civilization — a medical-history/humanities framing would need to genuinely fit a given cycle's call.
- **Guggenheim Fellowship [S]:** Individuals only, no affiliation required, mid-career, highly competitive (thousands apply, ~190 awards), fields include medicine/biology and nonfiction writing. Filed as an aspiration target once a manuscript is published and reviewed — not a near-term plan.
- **Wisconsin Arts Board [V/S, official FAQ]:** **As of now, individuals generally may NOT apply** for WAB grants (exception: Folk Arts Apprenticeship). WAB money reaches individuals mainly through **regranting partners**, and WAB lists outside funding opportunities (including PEN above). Honest verdict: check the regranting partner list for Douglas's region before spending effort here; do not assume a direct individual grant exists.

**AI field (credits/fellowships — mostly competitive or affiliation-leaning; verify status):**
- **OpenAI Researcher Access Program [S]:** API credits up to ~$1,000/12 months, quarterly review — the most realistic big-lab lane for a solo researcher; credits, not cash.

## 8. [FLAG] Taxes and benefits — factual cautions only, check before money moves

*This section is general information from public sources, not tax, legal, or benefits advice for Douglas. The decision step is a check with his FoodShare/benefits caseworker (and, for tax, a qualified preparer or the IRS pages cited) before relying on any of it.*

**Tax character depends on what the payment *is* [S, IRS-based]:**
- Money given out of "detached and disinterested generosity," with **nothing expected or received in return**, may be a **gift**, excluded from the recipient's gross income under 26 U.S.C. §102. Personal crowdfunding for needs is commonly analyzed this way.
- Money paid **in exchange for goods, services, perks, memberships with benefits, or products** (including pay-what-you-want downloads and reward tiers) looks like **income/business receipts** — reportable, potentially on Schedule C, potentially subject to self-employment tax.
- Calling a payment a "donation" or "tip" on a webpage **does not decide** its character; the facts do.
- **Donors** get **no charitable deduction** for gifts to an individual — deductions require a qualified charitable organization. Never tell a supporter otherwise.
- **Form 1099-K [S]:** Under the reinstated threshold reported in 2026 sources, payment platforms generally must file 1099-K only when payments for goods/services exceed **$20,000 AND 200 transactions** in a year. *Receiving or not receiving a form does not decide whether money is taxable* — and processors (Stripe/PayPal) issue forms under their own rules (Ko-fi itself does not issue 1099s; Stripe may).
- Practical consequence [R]: keep the §5 income log from the first dollar, keep tips (support) and shop sales (purchases) in separate columns, and keep both fields' money labeled by Goal.

**SNAP / FoodShare (Wisconsin) [V, Wisconsin policy handbooks]**
 [FLAG]:
- Wisconsin's FoodShare policy material treats **funds received through crowdfunding accounts (GoFundMe, Kickstarter, Indiegogo named as examples) that are accessible to the household as countable** — in asset sections as a liquid asset, and in a related Wisconsin EBD/Medicaid-style handbook section, crowdfunding funds are described as a **gift counted as unearned income in the month of receipt and as an available asset in later months while still in the person's possession**; funds **not accessible** at the time of interview are disregarded until withdrawn, then counted on withdrawal under that section.
- Wisconsin's BadgerCare Plus handbook takes a *different* position for that program (gifts, including crowdfunding, not counted as income) — **programs differ**, which is exactly why a program-specific check matters.
- Regular SNAP rules include an asset test for some households (historical Wisconsin material cites EBD-household asset limits; amounts are COLA-adjusted) while many FoodShare households are exempt from asset verification under broad-based categorical eligibility — **which set of rules applies to Douglas's own household is a caseworker question, not a thing this plan can settle.**
- **[FLAG] Action this creates:** Before the first campaign withdraws money in a pattern, Douglas asks his caseworker, in plain terms: *"If strangers send me small support payments through Ko-fi/GitHub Sponsors for my writing/research, how are those counted for my FoodShare — income in the month received, an asset after, or disregarded — and what do I need to report, and when?"* Ember can draft that question and the income log to show; the asking is his.

**Medicare [FLAG]:**
- Medicare (the age/disability health program) is **not** a means-tested benefit in the way SNAP/Medicaid are; premiums for Part B can, however, be income-related (IRMAA) based on tax-return income from two years prior, and *separate* programs that help with Medicare costs (Medicare Savings Programs, Extra Help, and any Medicaid overlap) **are** means-tested. Which of those, if any, touch Douglas is not recorded in this plan and must not be guessed.
- **[FLAG]** The same caseworker/benefits check should ask explicitly whether donation/support income affects *any* benefit Douglas actually receives — FoodShare, and any Medicare-cost-help or Medicaid-linked benefit by name — rather than assuming "Medicare" acts as one thing.

**Platform wording cautions [R]:**
- Never use "tax-deductible," "charity," "nonprofit," or "501(c)(3)" anywhere Douglas is the recipient. He is an individual.
- Keep manuscript framing creative/research — no donation copy may promise or imply medical benefit to a donor.
