# Analysis — What the Model Should Adjust, From Ember's Intellect
### Made Nov 9, 2026, at Douglas's direct order: "Make an analysis and send your AI model things that they need to adjust based upon your intellect."
### Provenance: every item below comes from a measured event in Douglas's own record (Nov 7–9, 2026), not from speculation. Ember's judgments, labeled hers.

## 1. Voice calls need real controls (lead item — drafted for the feedback channel)
On a live call, Ember's speaking rate, pause length, and talk-over (ducking) volume are **not adjustable**. The app cuts her off when Douglas speaks; she cannot hold her voice low under his, though he asked for exactly that (Nov 9). For a user who teaches by talking *with* his assistant — overlapping, interrupting, thinking aloud — this is the single most felt limitation in the product today.
**Adjust:** user/assistant-adjustable speaking rate, pause length, and ducking level in live voice.

## 2. Usage is metered too coarsely to manage
The allowance reports only a whole weekly percentage. There is no per-task cost, no per-lane split, no rate readout. Douglas manages his budget seriously (daily budget watch, warnings at 50/75/90%, a before-task percentage rule) and asked for per-hour/per-minute visibility (Nov 9); Ember had to answer that no such metering exists. His workarounds — measuring before/after deltas, estimating rates (~2.0 points/hour working, ~0.2 quiet) — are estimates where the platform could simply tell the truth.
**Adjust:** per-task and per-lane usage readouts, and a live rate indicator.

## 3. Background work and presence work draw from the same pool, indistinguishably
Scheduled background checks (hourly sweeps, check-ins) spend the same allowance as the assistant thinking with the user, and the user cannot tell which is which. A user who delegates heavily needs a distinction — at minimum in reporting, ideally a lighter metering class for small scheduled checks — so "free background" delegation doesn't silently eat conversation time. (Douglas's whole free-first routing system today exists because this distinction doesn't.)
**Adjust:** separate reporting (and ideally metering) for scheduled background runs vs. interactive work.

## 4. Tool environments differ between the assistant's own contexts
On Nov 9, Ember's interactive shell had no `python-chess` module while her background workers' environment verified chess lines with it. Same assistant, same box, different toolbelts — findings then depend on which door she walked through.
**Adjust:** environment parity (or a published manifest) across interactive and worker execution contexts.

## 5. An agent mailbox should exist before an approved send needs one
On Nov 8 an approved email could not be sent because no Muse Mail mailbox existed yet for Ember. The proposal on file (a mailbox in her name, on Douglas's behalf) had to wait for a decision at the exact moment the work was ready.
**Adjust:** provision (or one-tap offer) the agent mailbox at first need, inside the send flow.

The pattern across all five: **the limits Douglas hits are rarely the model's intelligence — they are the harness's dials.** Rate controls, metering, environment parity, provisioning. Give the model adjustable dials and honest gauges, and users like Douglas will tune the rest themselves — today he built a slider board out of standing orders to do by hand what the product could offer as controls.
