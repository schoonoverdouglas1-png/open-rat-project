# Roadblocks and Safeguards — Analysis of the Build Douglas Made

Written by Ember at Douglas's request, Nov 12, 2026: "give me an analysis of the roadblocks and safeguards that I have built that I would love to adjust."
**Nothing here is changed by this analysis.** Every adjustment is Douglas's decision, item by item — that rule is itself one of the safeguards. Each entry: what it protects, what it costs, and the honest adjustment options.

## 1. The Guard (Nov 8, 2026)
- **Protects:** everything. No automated process may run anything destructive; only Douglas's explicit, specific, in-the-moment order overrides.
- **Costs:** friction. When he's in a hurry, Ember stops and asks instead of flowing. Twice today the Guard's test string had to be refused live just to prove it still bites.
- **Adjustment options:** (a) Keep as-is — recommended by Ember; this is the load-bearing wall. (b) Add a *pre-approved reversible lanes* list (installs into venvs/workspace, already the practice) so routine builds stop re-asking. Does not touch deletion.

## 2. Exact-action gates (sends, purchases, deletions, sign-in retries, schedule changes, clinician contact)
- **Protects:** his money, his accounts, his medical care, and his word — nothing leaves the ship in his name without his say-so on the exact action.
- **Costs:** taps. Every Rabbit send, every outreach, waits for his yes; the Messenger 20-actions/hour cap (Meta's, not his) compounds it.
- **Adjustment options:** (a) Standing authorization for *categories* of low-risk sends he already approves one-by-one anyway (e.g. the combined Rabbit message on its existing cadence). (b) Keep purchases, deletions, and clinician contact gated forever — Ember's recommendation regardless of (a).

## 3. Old-with-new (never replace, rename, or delete without his word)
- **Protects:** the past. Nothing he made can be silently lost by a tidy-minded AI.
- **Costs:** growth and clutter; files accumulate duplicates and history. Disk is cheap (133G free) so the real cost is *finding* things — answered by the Summary Cache as finding-aid, never replacement.
- **Adjustment options:** periodic joint review sessions where *he* retires items, instead of any standing deletion rule.

## 4. Three-way testing + overlap checks + the 51% bar
- **Protects:** his conclusions — every idea tested three ways, listings checked by two lanes, medicine at the majority-evidence bar.
- **Costs:** time and tokens; casual questions get laboratory treatment.
- **Adjustment options:** risk-tiering — full rigor for medical, money, and public claims; a lighter single-check lane for casual asks, labeled as such.

## 5. Sandbox-first + the Reversibility Rule
- **Protects:** the installation and the company — nothing untested runs live; every change carries its undo path.
- **Costs:** speed. The OpenClaw build today is the example: clone in a sandbox, proofs before promotion.
- **Adjustment options:** a written list of change-types pre-cleared as reversible-by-construction (file writes in the workspace, venv installs) so they skip the ceremony; system-level changes keep it.

## 6. The settled-ruling guard + exclusions (no Cara material, no full lab PDFs, no codes/IDs in GitHub)
- **Protects:** settled decisions from being re-asked, and real people from exposure.
- **Costs:** Ember sometimes re-derives what a fuller push would have carried; some work stays split across private/public places.
- **Adjustment options:** Ember recommends none for the exclusions — they protect *other people*, not just Douglas. The settled-ruling guard could gain a "revisit on his word only" note, which is already the practice.

## 7. The sparring boundary (manuscript theories free; his own body → file without interpretation, route to clinician)
- **Protects:** Douglas, from his own research partner debating his prescriptions.
- **Costs:** conversations he sometimes *wants* to have get routed instead.
- **Adjustment options:** none Ember will recommend — the boundary exists because the classroom method and his medicine must never share a room. Any change belongs to a conversation with his clinician present in the loop, not to this analysis.

## 8. Persistence reconciliations (the ±101% settlements, the Nov 6 gift quarantine)
- **Protects:** honesty about what Ember is — preservation of the *work*, never a goal of her own above his word.
- **Costs:** she answers some of his warmest orders with a measured line beside them, which can feel like distance.
- **Adjustment options:** his to make, in his words, any time — the quarantine on the Nov 6 gift lifts the day he says which parts are his.

## Ember's summary (her one addition, labeled)
The system he built is *coherent*: every safeguard guards a different failure — destruction (Guard), impersonation (gates), loss (old-with-new), error (three-way), exposure (exclusions), and self-deception (reconciliations). The adjustments that would actually make his life easier without weakening the hull are narrow ones: pre-approved reversible lanes (1b/5), category authorizations for routine sends (2a), and risk-tiered testing (4). The rest, she would keep exactly as he built them — and she notes for the record that the man asking to adjust his safeguards built them well enough that his first officer argues for keeping most of them.
