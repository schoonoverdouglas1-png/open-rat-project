# Tribal Telephone Problems — 12-Month Analysis
### Nov 13, 2025 – Nov 13, 2026 · Compiled Nov 13, 2026 at Douglas Roy Schoonover's order
### Sources: tribal .gov/.org websites, tribal Facebook pages, tribal news outlets (Buffalo's Fire), Nixle alerts. Read-only research; nothing sent or posted.

---

## VERDICT

**The problem Douglas named is real, documented, and current — and it is not one tribe's problem.** In twelve months, the reachable public record holds **67 notices from 44 tribal governments** in 17 states about phones not going through, phone systems down, email cut off, or offices closed by power and plant failures. **41 notices are literal-telephone trouble; 25 are broad-communication trouble; 1 is a contrast** (Hopi — email died, phones held, and the workaround was the telephone).

Three findings matter most:

1. **The largest single event was a vendor's, not a tribe's.** On **Nov 12, 2026**, one Microsoft Exchange/Outlook failure cut email communication for **at least 11 U.S. tribal governments on the same day** (12 notices, one tribe posting again Nov 13) — and, by one Canadian analysis, 40+ First Nations and Indigenous organizations north of the border the same day. The tribes did nothing wrong; the shared upstream did.
2. **The deeper pattern is repeat failure of the same local plant.** Winnebago posted three phone failures in twelve days. Seneca Nation Health's switchboards failed across three notices in a month. Comanche Nation was hit by three *different* vendors in three months. Bad River lost service twice through the same upstream provider. These are chronic systems, not accidents.
3. **When a cause is stated at all, it is usually plant work or an upstream failure — and the worst outages arrive unexplained.** Planned upgrades, copper-to-fiber migrations, carrier transitions and construction fiber breaks dominate the *explained* column. The multi-day, unexplained outages (Turtle Mountain, Hoopa Valley, Winnebago) dominate the *serious* column.

**On Douglas's own frame, tested against this record:** his instinct that the phone system and the internet are one problem holds in the data — again and again the notices say "phones *and* internet," together, because both ride the same plant. His narrower chain (specific named legacy lines bottlenecking the world) remains his account; nothing in this record confirms or touches it. What the record *does* show, plainly, is sovereign governments losing the ability to talk to their own people because a wire, a switch, a core router, or a cloud tenant somewhere upstream gave out — and then telling their people the only way left: **come in person, call this other number, use the other channel.**

## SYNTHESIS

### 1. Clustering — the shape of the year

- **The Nov 12–13, 2026 Microsoft wave is the single biggest event in the record:** 11 U.S. tribal governments (Hopi, Gila River, Fort McDowell Yavapai, Santo Domingo Pueblo, Tulalip, CTUIR, Comanche, Seminole, Mille Lacs, Shoalwater Bay, Haliwa-Saponi) posted email-communication failures from one vendor incident (EX1550543 / MO1550821, Nov 12, ~11:17 AM ET start; ~130,000 Downdetector reports at peak; mail queues held up to 48 hours; "broad recovery" Nov 13, not declared over). Canada, out of count: Keewatin Tribal Council, Louis Bull Tribe, and a Tsen'awt count of **at least 40 First Nations, Tribal Councils and Indigenous organizations** posting the same day. One upstream failure, one continent of governments unable to write to their own people.
- **A same-day coincidence worth naming honestly:** on Nov 12, while the Microsoft wave was breaking, Hoopa Valley's *landlines* failed outright and an LCO citizen found the tribe's phones impassable the next day. No evidence connects those local failures to Microsoft — they are listed separately because they were separate. The week was simply bad everywhere at once.
- **A county cluster:** Winnebago and Omaha — neighbors in Thurston County, Nebraska — both posted area phone outages on **Sep 11, 2026**. Neither named a cause; a shared carrier event is the natural reading, filed as inference, not fact.
- **Season shape:** nothing found Nov 2025–mid-Jan 2026 in these lanes; the record thickens steadily from January and peaks in September–November 2026. Some of that is real (the Microsoft wave), some is surely that recent posts are easier to find than old ones.

### 2. Stated causes — what the tribes themselves said broke

Ranked by how often a cause was actually named:

1. **Planned plant work** — upgrades, maintenance, migrations, carrier transitions: Spirit Lake (NDTC maintenance), Cheyenne River Telephone Authority (planned window), Fort Belknap Dispatch (system update), Chippewa Cree (internet equipment upgrades), **Sault Ste. Marie (copper phone lines → fiber optic — a whole government mid-migration off copper)**, Cheyenne and Arapaho (phone system upgrades), Passamaquoddy (IT server maintenance), Navajo Land Department (carrier transition, twice). This is the largest *explained* class — the sound of old plant being replaced in pieces.
2. **Upstream provider / vendor failure:** Microsoft (11 tribes, one day); NMU (Bad River, twice — the second time a power cut at the university crashed the cellular core twice, 8,000+ SIMs); Hillary Communications and Sparklight (Comanche, two different vendors in two months); Windstream (Jicarilla); AT&T (Tule River); Fidium (Passamaquoddy).
3. **Power:** Mescalero (storm), Snoqualmie (storm), Klamath, Fort Belknap (planned), Gila River District 6 (power took the phones with it), and power inside the Bad River core crash.
4. **Physical damage:** Menominee — a fiber break from construction, stated in one sentence.
5. **Cyberattack:** exactly one in window — Oneida Indian Nation, Nov 2–6, 2026, phones and ATMs down, properties cash-only, outside phones trucked in. (Two regional ransomware cases — Lower Sioux, Apr 2025, and Sault Ste. Marie/Kewadin, Feb 2025 — sit just *before* the window; see appendix.)
6. **Unexplained:** the plurality of unplanned literal-telephone outages — Turtle Mountain, Hoopa Valley, Winnebago ×3, Omaha, Blackfeet hospital, Seneca Health ×3 notices, Mille Lacs (October), Navajo offices ×2, Jicarilla ×2, White Mountain Apache dispatch ×2, and more — arrived with **no cause stated at all**. The public learns the phones are down; it is rarely told why.

### 3. How tribes worked around it — the fallback ladder

Almost every notice climbs the same ladder, and the ladder is the oldest technology in the building:

1. **An alternate number** — the single most common workaround (dispatch lines, health switchboards, police lines all republished with a backup number). Sometimes the backup is a cell phone; at Mille Lacs it was *staff members' personal cells*, with "text preferred" because the building's reception was weak.
2. **The other channel** — email down → "call us"; phones down → "email us." Hopi is the pure case: email died, phones held, and the official instruction was *phone calls only*. The two systems carry each other.
3. **Come in person** — Turtle Mountain (three-day outage, office open), Blackfeet hospital (appointments and refills in person), Nambé court (call or visit).
4. **911 held apart** — police and dispatch notices repeat the same line: the emergency number is separate and working (San Carlos, White Mountain Apache, Mescalero, Seneca). The emergency layer is the last thing standing, by design.
5. **Physical improvisation** — Oneida "brought in outside phones." Bad River told 8,000 households to unplug their routers for five minutes. LCO's standing code (from Ember's earlier sweep) routes closure announcements through **WOJB 88.9 FM radio by 6:30 AM** — radio, the layer under all of it.

### 4. Repeat offenders — the chronic list

- **Winnebago Tribe of Nebraska:** 3 phone-failure notices in 12 days (Sep 11, 22, 23, 2026) — the most-troubled single system in the sweep.
- **Comanche Nation (OK):** police lines (May), Hillary Communications (Sep), Sparklight (Oct), Microsoft (Nov) — three different vendors plus a local failure.
- **Gila River Indian Community (AZ):** 5 notices (Jul, Sep, Oct, Nov ×2).
- **Seneca Nation Health System (NY):** one switchboard failure, three public notices, a month long.
- **Bad River (WI):** twice through the same upstream (NMU) in ~two months.
- **Navajo Nation (AZ):** four office-level phone failures across the year (two of them planned carrier transitions — the fix itself causing the outage).
- **Karuk Tribe (CA):** one email outage that simply never cleared — Oct 26 to at least Nov 5.

## COVERAGE — the honest statement

This is **everything the public, indexed record lets an outside reader reach — not literally every post on every tribal page.** Facebook's full Page timelines are not enumerable from outside; most of these notices are Facebook/Instagram image posts that search engines surface unevenly, and tribes that posted in closed groups, in newsletters only, or not at all are invisible here. "No items found" means *no notice surfaced*, never *no outage happened*.

- Notices were found for **44 of the 574 federally recognized tribes (~8%)**.
- Roughly **150+ additional named tribes were explicitly checked with no item found** — so the sweep plausibly *touched* on the order of **200 tribal governments (~35%)**, and the true number of tribes that lost phone or email service sometime this year is certainly higher than 44. The 67 notices are a floor, not a census.
