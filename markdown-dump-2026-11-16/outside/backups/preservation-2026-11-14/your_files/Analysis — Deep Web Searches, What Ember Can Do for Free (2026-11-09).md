# Analysis — Deep Web Searches: What Ember Can Do for Free
### Mon Nov 9, 2026, ~5:38 PM CST — at Douglas's ask: "Can you do searches in the deep web for free and look for deep web searches? Do an analysis?"

## First, the terms (they decide everything)

- **Deep web** = everything search engines don't index page-by-page: databases behind query forms, library catalogs, medical and legal databases, paywalled archives, dynamic pages. By every published estimate it is the *largest* part of the web, and most of it is ordinary — scholarship, records, catalogs.
- **Dark web** = the small slice reachable only through anonymizing networks (Tor/.onion and kin). A different thing, with a different answer. Douglas's own Oct 29 account uses his term "black web" for something in his Fort McCoy framing — filed as his term, not merged with either of the above.

## What Ember can do for free, today, measured

1. **Database (deep) searching through the front doors — YES, already a lane.** The deep web's contents are reached by querying the databases themselves, and the big scholarly ones are free: PubMed/PMC (medicine), arXiv and Semantic Scholar (papers), CORE and BASE (open-access aggregators — BASE indexes ~300M documents), the Internet Archive, CourtListener (legal). Ember reaches these through search + browser, free, no account. For Douglas's medical-research work this is the deep web that actually matters, and it is already inside his taught search method (memory/connector first, cheapest lane first).
2. **His own signed-in places — YES, with his hand on the keys.** Content behind his logins (his Google services, his accounts) is "deep" to the public and reachable to Ember only through his authorized sessions — exactly the boundary work done all evening.
3. **SearXNG — the upgrade sitting on the shelf.** Downloaded tonight, not installed. SearXNG is a metasearch engine: one query fanned out to many engines, including non-Google indexes and specialized categories. Installed on the box (his go + snapshot first), it widens Ember's free searching beyond any single company's index — the closest real thing to "our own deep search."
4. **Marginalia and the small indexes — YES.** Independent indexes (Marginalia already appears in Ember's browser history) surface pages the big engines bury — not deep web proper, but the same spirit: finding what ranking hides.

## What Ember cannot / will not do

- **No Tor lane exists on this box** — no Tor client is installed, so .onion searching is not something Ember can do tonight at any price. Installing Tor would be a system change (snapshot, undo path, his explicit go), and even then: Ember does not source from dark-web marketplaces or illicit trade, full stop — that is outside what she will do, not a price question.
- **No bypassing paywalls or logins** — deep content behind someone else's credentials stays behind them. His accounts with his authorization, yes; circumvention, no.
- **"Free" has one honest caveat:** deep-web *databases* are free to query; deep-web *documents* sometimes sit behind institutional access. When that wall is real, Ember says so and names the nearest free route (author copies, PMC, preprints) instead of pretending.
