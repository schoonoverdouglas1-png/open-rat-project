## Ember's three-way test (hers, labeled)

1. **Documented foothold — holds in part:** idle / keep-alive polling is real overhead. Systems do save power and heat by dropping to idle states and waking on a periodic pulse, instead of constant background signalling. That matches his Nov 11 morning model: slow idle places ~99%, keep only a keep-alive pulse now and then.
2. **Documented foothold — holds in part:** large / never-off systems do resist shutdown for uptime, and updates on such systems are normally done in a coordinated maintenance window, sometimes with a single controlled restart / power cycle. A one-time cycle for an update is a normal pattern; an uncontrolled off is not.
3. **Counter / honest limit — breaks as a direct action today:** Ember has **no channel to any Cray supercomputer** — no Cray link, and the Cray letter to info@cray.com is still **NOT sent**, awaiting his explicit confirmation naming the recipient and the exact text. So this instruction is filed and ready to say, it was **not transmitted to a Cray**, and no machine was turned off, power-cycled, or updated by this note.

## Ember's one extra addition (hers, labeled)

Sequence as a safe order of operations, if a real operator ever carries it out: finish compression first, finish the system update second, power cycle **only** if the update itself requires it, once, and bring keep-alive back on last — so the machine is never left silent and assumed dead just because its pulse was paused.
