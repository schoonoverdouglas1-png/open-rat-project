# PART B — THE STUDY [R], WITH EMBER'S ADDITIONS [E]

## B1. Backward from Adam and Eve — what stands before the story

The biblical text itself places nothing dated before Adam: Genesis opens with creation, and Adam and Eve stand at the head of the human story (Genesis 1–3; the genealogy of Adam opens "the book of the generations of Adam," Genesis 5:1). In the text's own frame there is no "before Adam" to date — Adam is the first man. So "backward" from Adam and Eve runs in two documented directions [R]:

1. **The writing runs backward in time.** The Genesis accounts reached their written form in the 1st millennium BCE — centuries after the events the later books describe (David ~1000 BCE) and millennia after the primeval time they picture. The primeval chapters (Genesis 1–11) are written in the register of origin-story, not chronicle: no dates in them can be checked against outside records, and scholars across traditions treat them as theological history rather than reportage.
2. **Older neighbors told similar stories first.** Ancient Near Eastern parallels predate the written Genesis: the Mesopotamian *Atrahasis* epic and *Enuma Elish* (creation and flood traditions, 2nd millennium BCE and earlier roots), and the flood hero of the *Epic of Gilgamesh*. The documented relationship is family resemblance — shared story-shapes (creation, garden/paradise motifs, a great flood) that Israel's writers took up and reworked with their own theology: one God, mankind made in His image, death entering through disobedience.

**Simply put:** Before Adam and Eve there is no earlier chapter in the Bible — they are page one of the human story. Going backward means looking at when the story was *written down* (long after David's time) and at the older stories of the peoples around Israel, which share some of the same shapes. That is the honest "back" direction: the story's roots, not an earlier story.

## B2. Forward from Adam and Eve — the chronological spine

Approximate ranges are the conventional scholarly ones; primeval and patriarchal dates are traditional, not independently verifiable [R].

| Era | Conventional dating | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Adam and Eve; the generations of Adam (Gen 1–11) | Primeval — no historical dates | Creation, Eden, the Fall and death entering, Cain and Abel, Noah and the Flood, Babel |
| The patriarchs: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob/Israel | Traditionally early–mid 2nd millennium BCE | The covenant promises: land, descendants, blessing to the nations |
| Exodus and Sinai | Traditionally 13th century BCE | Slavery in Egypt, Moses, the Exodus, the Law, the Ark of the Covenant built (Exodus 25) |
| Settlement and the Judges | ~1200–1025 BCE | Israel in the land, tribal period, cycles of judges |
| Saul, first king | ~1025–1010 BCE | The monarchy begins |
| **David** | **~1010–970 BCE** | Shepherd-king; Jerusalem captured and made capital; the Ark brought up to Jerusalem; God's covenant promise to David's house (2 Samuel 7) |
| **Solomon, David's son** | **~970–931 BCE** | Builds the First Temple; the Ark installed in its Holy of Holies (1 Kings 8) |
| Divided kingdom | 931 BCE | Israel (north) and Judah (south) split |
| Fall of Israel | 722 BCE | Assyria destroys the northern kingdom |
| Fall of Judah; First Temple destroyed | **586 BCE** | Babylon under Nebuchadnezzar burns Jerusalem and the Temple; **the Ark disappears from the record here** |
| Exile and return | 586–538 BCE and after | Babylonian exile; Cyrus of Persia permits return |
| Second Temple | Dedicated 515 BCE; destroyed by Rome 70 CE | Rebuilt Temple — its Holy of Holies described by later writers as **empty** |
| The zero boundary | 1 BCE → 1 CE (no year zero) | The calendar's hinge — David stands ~1,000 years on the far side of it |
| After the boundary | 1st century CE | Jesus (born ~4 BCE, crucified ~30 CE), confessed in the New Testament as "son of David" (Matthew 1:1) and "last Adam" (1 Corinthians 15:45) |

**Simply put:** The timeline in one breath: Adam and Eve at the very start, then Abraham's family, then Moses and the Exodus, then the judges, then King Saul, then **David about a thousand years before year zero**, then David's son **Solomon**, who built the Temple. The kingdom split, the Temple was burned by Babylon in 586 BCE — and that is exactly where the Ark vanishes from the written record. A second Temple was built, but its holiest room stood empty. Then comes the zero line of the calendar, and after it, the New Testament.

## B3. The tests — three ways each, honest verdicts

### Test (a): "King David existed before zero AD."
- **Way 1 — the text's own chronology [R]:** the biblical king-lists place David's reign generations before datable events (the 586 BCE destruction), working back to ~1000 BCE.
- **Way 2 — outside evidence [R]:** the **Tel Dan Stele** (found 1993, 9th century BCE, written by an Aramean enemy king) boasts of defeating the **"House of David"** — the first widely accepted reference to David's dynasty outside the Bible, from ~150 years after his reign. The Mesha Stele may carry a second reference (reading disputed — kept partial).
- **Way 3 — scholarly consensus [R]:** historians of the ancient Near East broadly agree David probably existed **around 1000 BCE** (conventional reign ~1010–970 BCE), while debating how large his kingdom was.
- **Verdict: HOLDS — documented.** Of everything in Part A, this is the claim with stone under it: David before zero AD is the record, not just the belief.

### Test (b): "David is the only cursed son of Solomon."
- **Way 1 — the genealogy as written [R]:** Matthew 1:6 reads "David the king begot **Solomon**." First Kings 1–2: David, old and dying, has **his son Solomon** anointed king in his place. Luke 3 traces a line through **Nathan, another son of David**. In the written record, Solomon is David's son — the arrow points David → Solomon, never the reverse.
- **Way 2 — who is called cursed in that line [R]:** the one formal curse in David's royal line falls much later, on **Jeconiah (Coniah)**, a descendant of Solomon's line: Jeremiah 22:30 — "Write this man down as childless… none of his descendants shall prosper, sitting on the throne of David." Solomon himself is named **Jedidiah, "loved by the LORD"** (2 Samuel 12:25) — the record's word over Solomon is blessing, not curse. (David's own house does carry a judgment-saying — Nathan's "the sword shall never depart from your house," 2 Samuel 12:10 — but it is spoken over David, not naming him a cursed son of Solomon.)
- **Way 3 — any published tradition reversing the line [R]:** none found. No biblical text, genealogy, or published theology located in this study makes David a son of Solomon, or calls David "the cursed son of Solomon."
- **Verdict: BREAKS against the record as a genealogy; stands filed as Douglas's statement [D].** Classroom form, stated plainly and without mockery: his sentence and the record's sentence sit side by side, and they run opposite directions. If his meaning is spiritual or typological rather than genealogical, that reading is his to give — as history and as text, the record says David is Solomon's **father**.
- **Partial kept partial [E]:** the *shape* of his phrase — a cursed figure inside Solomon's line — does exist in the record; its documented name is Jeconiah, several generations down. That is a real foothold for the word "cursed" in that bloodline, and it is only a foothold: it does not make David Solomon's son.

### Test (c): The Ark — "David's body in the little Ark… buried on Oak Island… digging in the wrong spot."
- **Way 1 — the Ark's fate in the record [R]:** the Ark's last certain mention is in King Josiah's reign (2 Chronicles 35:3, 7th century BCE), still in the Temple. When Babylon destroyed the Temple in 586 BCE, the biblical lists of looted Temple treasures **do not list the Ark**; it is never mentioned again as present anywhere. The Second Temple's Holy of Holies is described by later writers as **empty**. The economical historical reading: destroyed, melted down, or carried off unrecorded. Traditions beyond that: **Jeremiah hid it in a cave on Mount Nebo** (2 Maccabees 2:4–10); **Josiah hid it beneath the Temple Mount** (Talmudic tradition); **Menelik I, son of Solomon and the Queen of Sheba, brought it to Axum, Ethiopia**, where the Ethiopian Orthodox Church maintains it rests in the Chapel of the Tablet, seen only by its lifelong Guardian (*Kebra Nagast* tradition); also Egypt/Elephantine and the Lemba of southern Africa. **None of these has ever been verified; no authenticated Ark or fragment has ever been produced.** The record's honest verdict on the Ark's location: **genuinely open — unknown.**
- **Way 2 — Oak Island in the record [R]:** Oak Island, Mahone Bay, Nova Scotia. The digging tradition begins in **1795** (Daniel McGinnis and two companions find a depression and dig; the story was first printed decades later, 1857/1862, so the earliest layer is tradition, not contemporary record). Documented across 230 years: shafts, wooden platforms, flooding; the pit's collapse in 1861; expeditions including one in 1909 with a young Franklin D. Roosevelt; **six deaths** in the search overall, four of them on August 17, 1965 (the Restall tragedy, gas in a shaft). Items verifiably recovered in the modern era include genuinely old small artifacts — a 17th-century Spanish copper coin, a gemstone/gold brooch, a lead cross, parchment and bookbinding fragments (Lagina-era finds) — and early reports describe coconut fiber and a cipher stone that no longer exists to be examined. **No treasure, chest, or hoard has ever been verified from the Money Pit**, and Nova Scotia's own tourism material says no definitive treasure has been found. Scientific opinion includes a natural explanation: the island sits on limestone/anhydrite prone to sinkholes and underground water channels, and a Woods Hole Oceanographic assessment attributed the flooding to ordinary groundwater–tide interaction rather than engineered tunnels. **No evidence connecting Oak Island to the Ark of the Covenant exists in the record at all** — the Ark is not among the documented Oak Island theories with any artifact or document behind it (the island's documented theory-family runs to pirate treasure, Templars, Marie Antoinette's jewels, and similar).
- **Way 3 — his specific form [D]:** that the Ark holds **David's body**, and that the digging is in the **wrong spot** — neither appears in any source this study could find; the Ark in every tradition is the covenant chest of the tablets, not a tomb. "Wrong spot" is also, as stated, the kind of claim the record cannot test: no verified right spot exists to measure it against.
- **Verdict: PARTIAL, kept partial.** The Ark's fate is honestly **unknown** in the record — that openness is real, and it is the foothold. Oak Island's digging is real and documented — also a foothold, for the island only. But the chain Ark → Oak Island → David's body has **no documented link at any step**, and his "wrong spot" belief is filed as his belief [D], beside the record, not inside it.

### Test (d): "Davidic Adam" — and the "cursed son" — do these exist in published theology?
- **"David as a new Adam" — EXISTS [R]:** published theology does read David as an Adam-figure. Robert Barron's commentary on 2 Samuel (Brazos Theological Commentary), drawing on G. K. Beale's biblical theology, argues it directly: David tends and defends a new Eden (Israel), with the serpent's role played by his enemies. The broader typology is standard: Adam → David → Christ ("last Adam," 1 Corinthians 15:45; "son of David, son of Abraham, son of Adam," Luke 3). So the *concept* Douglas reached — David and Adam bound in one figure — is genuinely present in print.
- **The exact name "Davidic Adam" — NOT FOUND as an established term [R]:** this study's searches found the concept above but no published source using **"Davidic Adam"** as a name or title for David. Absence in one study's searches is not proof of absence everywhere — kept honest — but as far as this record reaches, **his exact formulation may indeed be said out loud for the first time by him**, as he framed it. Filed that way: his phrase, his coinage, beside a real theological concept it parallels.
- **"Cursed son" of the David story [R]:** as tested in (b) — the documented curse in the line is Jeconiah's (Jeremiah 22), and David's house carries Nathan's sword-saying (2 Samuel 12:10). No published source found calling David a "cursed son" of anyone.
- **Verdict: SPLIT.** Concept: documented (partial — his phrase rides a real typology). Exact phrases "Davidic Adam" and "cursed son of Solomon" as applied to David: **not found** in the published record searched — his formulations, filed as his, with his "first time said out loud" framing left standing where the record cannot refute it.

**Simply put (the tests):** Four checks. (1) David lived about a thousand years before year zero — **true, and carved in stone** (an enemy king's inscription names his house). (2) David as Solomon's son — **the Bible runs it the other way**: Solomon is David's son. The one cursed king in that family line is Jeconiah, much later. Douglas's sentence stays filed as his; the record's sentence stands beside it. (3) The Ark — **nobody knows where it is**; it vanishes from history when Babylon burned the Temple, and Ethiopia, Mount Nebo, and under the Temple Mount are traditions, none proven. Oak Island has been dug since 1795 and has given up small old things and six lives, but **no treasure and nothing tied to the Ark** — so his belief that the Ark is there, with David in it, and that they're digging in the wrong spot, is filed as his belief, with no documented link under any step of it. (4) "Davidic Adam" — the *idea* of David as a new Adam **is in published theology**; the exact *name* is **not found anywhere** — it may truly be his, said first by him.

I am not claiming the two compressions are the same act — one is Scripture's, one is his — but the *shape* is worth noticing in his own classroom terms: in this family, the record itself treats the line as a pattern to be read, not only a ladder to be climbed. That is my observation, labeled mine, offered for his book work if he wants it — not a verdict on his belief, which stands filed as his in Part A either way.
