## The logic of the situation — how it is written

- A track reference is written **[A]**, **[B]**, **[C]**… — one letter in brackets. Example: **[C]** pneumonia-group entry, **[A]** covered by PCV20, **[P]** partial.
- Track letters continue **I, J, L, M, N, O, Q, R, T, U, V, W, Y, Z** for future tracks — skipping **K, P, S, X**, which are reserved above, so no letter ever means two things.
- **When letters run out, even numbers take over** (Douglas's rule): the next tracks are **[2]**, **[4]**, **[6]**, **[8]**, **[10]**, **[12]**… — even numbers only, in order, forever. There is always another code; the system cannot run out.
- A full written reference combines them: **[Track letter] + [Word code] + status mark**, e.g. `C-DIS ●` = a Diseases-track entry, fully documented.

## Status marks (symbols, so they never collide with track letters)

- **●** Full — documented at the bar
- **◐** Partial — a partial relationship, reported as partial, never upgraded
- **○** GAP — empty at the bar; a question, not a claim

## Ember's additions (labeled hers, continuously — permitted more than one, Nov 11)

1. **Color is the artwork; the code is the logic.** Douglas loves colored text — "the most beautiful artwork to a computer guy" (his words) — and loves black and white the same. So color is layered on top for the eye and the heart, while letters, numbers, and marks carry the meaning in any medium, including a plain black-and-white page or a voice call where no color can be seen at all. Nothing is ever lost when the color is absent.
2. **Colors sometimes, on purpose.** Douglas's ruling today: if colors cost slightly more, use them sometimes anyway. Practice: colored where a table is meant to be looked at and kept (like this one); plain where speed or a long build makes plain wiser — his call and Ember's judgment together, case by case.
