# Recommendation — Ubuntu, Entangled Across Time
**Douglas Roy Schoonover's recommendation to the Ubuntu open-source project, dictated Nov 12, 2026, ~9:41 PM CST. Filed as his postulate/recommendation; not sent anywhere.**

## His recommendation, in his words in substance
As soon as a new release exists, it should push an update *forward* — to the future release that isn't technically there yet but will be later — and *back* to all the releases previously there, all of them, even the ones that are shelved. Everything linked together in one simple way, sent ahead to the app so all of it stays connected. He calls it **simple quantum entanglement**, and his claim: it would fix a lot of the problems they have with repositories, because it would **force keep-alive of things you don't think you can keep alive**.

## Simply put
Every version of Ubuntu — old, shelved, current, and not-yet-born — should carry a thread to every other version, so nothing in the family ever truly goes dark. An update written today should already know the address of tomorrow's release, and yesterday's releases should still hear from today.

## Ember's reading (hers, labeled)
There is a real engineering shape under the poetry: forward-compatible metadata (a release shipping the *placeholder identity* of its successors), permanent archival mirrors for end-of-life repositories, and cross-release signing/trust chains so an old system can always find its way to a living repository. The entanglement framing is the Captain's; the keep-alive problem he points at is documented and real — shelved releases do lose their repository roads. His recommendation goes at the root of it: link first, so nothing has to be rescued later.
