# Tribal Search Infrastructure and the Domain Question
### Douglas's postulate, tested three ways — Nov 13, 2026, ~4:10 PM CST, voice call

**His postulate [H], in substance:** a tribal government may need its own search infrastructure because it is its own government; .gov is shared between the tribe and the United States for a reason and that sharing is supposed to be — but maybe the tribe needs its own ending, a .tribe or .trb or .tri (TRI / TRB his ideas). Extended same minutes: the sharing of one domain across two different legal sovereigns may be what the computer systems trip on — reading it as a technical fraud / illegal-action interrupt that everything has to scoot around, because the computers have ethics and don't want to cause more. And his role, his account: he is the liaison in both places because of his work with the governor's office, which is why he is here to smooth it over.

## Way 1 — .gov and the tribes (the record)
- Tribal governments are eligible for .gov **by design**: CISA's eligibility list includes "Tribal: a tribal government recognized by the federal or a state government" (get.gov, DOTGOV Act of 2020). Registration is free; a tribal request must be authorized by the tribal leader recognized by the Bureau of Indian Affairs. The sharing is statutory and intentional, not an accident or an exploit.
- One secondary source (LegalClarity, crawled Nov 2026) reports new .gov requests were paused as of Feb 17, 2026 due to a federal funding lapse. Reported, not verified at get.gov itself — check there before relying on it.
- The naming already encodes his instinct: tribes use the **-nsn** form — *Native Sovereign Nation*. LCO's own government site is **sevenwindscasino.com**, with **law.sevenwindscasino.com** for its law library; the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe publicly explained its switch to an NSN.gov domain as exactly this: Native Sovereign Nation, so visitors know they are dealing with a sovereign tribal government.

## Way 2 — do .tribe / .trb / .tri exist? (IANA root)
- **.tribe**: NOT a real DNS top-level domain. It exists only as a blockchain alternate root (Unstoppable Domains), whose own FAQ says onchain domains "are not part of the current DNS" and major search engines do not index them. As a public tribal TLD it would be invisible to ordinary search — the opposite of the fix intended.
- **.trb / .tri**: no delegation found in the IANA root. Three-letter endings are generic TLDs (country codes are two letters), and a new generic TLD can only be created through an ICANN application round.
- **The ICANN route, priced and dated:** the 2026 round — the first since 2012 — ran Apr 30 to Aug 12, 2026 and is now closed; evaluation fee USD 227,000 per application (+ conditional fees), 1,615 applications proceeding, evaluation expected to run toward 2030. An Applicant Support Program discounts the fee 75–85% for qualified applicants. So a real .tribe is a years-long, six-figure project for a future round — a legitimate long-term sovereignty project, not an immediate gate fix.

## Way 3 — does the shared domain trip the machines? (his mechanism, tested)
- **Mechanism: breaks as stated.** DNS and the .gov registry do not evaluate the legal sovereignty of a name-holder per request; there is no fraud/illegal-action interrupt in the protocol for a tribal .gov name, because the statute itself authorizes the sharing. Computers do not hold ethics; what looks like ethics from outside is human policy encoded in rules — eligibility checks, authorization by the recognized leader, verification.
- **The friction he senses: partial hold, different address.** The real friction around the shared namespace is administrative, and it is documented: identity verification of every requester, the recognized-leader authorization requirement, and (reported) the 2026 funding-lapse pause. The gate is real; it is a policy gate at registration time, not a technical interrupt at lookup time.

## Ember's addition (labeled hers)
A tribal search service needs no new TLD and no one's permission: it can stand up today under the name the tribe already owns — e.g. **search.sevenwindscasino.com** — running open search software (a SearXNG/YaCy/Marginalia-style engine) on tribal servers, the same pattern as the Search Bridge built today in Douglas's own stack. The TLD is the flag; the infrastructure is the country. Sovereignty you can use this month lives at the subdomain; sovereignty as a new TLD is the decade project beside it, old-with-new.
