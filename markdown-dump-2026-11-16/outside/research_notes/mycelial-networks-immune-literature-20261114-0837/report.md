# Fungal Mycelial Networks and Human Health — Literature Foundations

*Prepared 2026-11-14 for a creative-scientific manuscript project. Sources read 2026-11-14. Flags: **verified live** = full text / abstract opened and read in the research browser on that date; **index** = search-index abstract/snippet only. This is a literature survey, not medical advice.*

## Summary

The literature supports a real, specific, and interesting story — and it is narrower than the popular version.

1. **Fungal β-glucans genuinely engage human innate immunity, through named receptors.** That mechanism (Dectin-1 → Syk → CARD9–BCL10–MALT1, plus complement receptor 3) is established by receptor identification, knockout mice, and human inborn errors of immunity. What is *not* established to the same standard is that eating mushroom supplements produces clinically important benefit in healthy people: human trials are small, heterogeneous, inconsistent at the cellular/molecular level, and the strongest cancer data belong to two purified pharmaceutical preparations (PSK and, more weakly, lentinan) used as adjuncts in Japan, not to whole mushrooms or mycelium products.
2. **Mycelia really do transport resources, remodel themselves as networks, produce chemical quorum signals (in yeasts), and show action-potential-*like* electrical activity.** Whether the electrical spikes are *communication* — let alone a language, or anything neuron-like in function — is unresolved and contested. The best 2025 review says the communication function was never irrefutably shown.
3. **Encapsulated fungi in immunology means, essentially, one genus: *Cryptococcus*.** Its glucuronoxylomannan capsule is the textbook case of a fungus hiding its immunogenic cell wall from pattern-recognition receptors. Other pathogens teach the same lesson by different means (e.g. *Candida* masking β-glucan under mannan, *Histoplasma* covering itself in α-1,3-glucan) — but they are not, strictly, encapsulated.
4. **“Fungal networks as a model for whole-body medicine” has no peer-reviewed clinical literature behind it.** No trial, no validated framework, no journal literature was found for it as a medical model. It is a metaphor built by stacking three true-but-limited findings (immune receptors for fungal sugars; network transport in soil fungi; distributed sensing without a brain) and then transferring properties across them. Used *as* metaphor in a manuscript it is defensible; presented *as* established medicine it is not.

## Findings

### 1. Fungal beta-glucans and other fungal polysaccharides — immune modulation: mechanisms and strength of evidence

#### Established

**What β-glucans are, and why source matters.** β-Glucans are a heterogeneous family of glucose polymers, not a single molecule. Fungal/yeast glucans typically have a β-(1,3) backbone with β-(1,6) branches; mushrooms have short β-(1,6) branches, yeast has longer β-(1,6) side branches with additional β-(1,3) regions; cereal (oat/barley) glucans are β-(1,3)/(1,4) and are **not** recognised by Dectin-1. Activity depends on branching frequency, molecular weight, solubility, particle size and conformation (single/triple helix), and the review literature explicitly warns that “what is observed for one preparation of glucan is often inappropriately extrapolated to all glucans” and that systematic structure–function studies are generally lacking. Particulate glucans are the optimal form for inducing innate immune memory; low-molecular-weight glucans such as laminarin do not favour a high response.
— Han et al., *Front Immunol* 2020, **verified live**: http://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7188827/

**The receptor mechanism is real and named.** β-(1,3)-glucan is a key pathogen-associated molecular pattern (PAMP). Its principal receptors are:
- **Dectin-1 (CLEC7A)**, a C-type lectin — identified as the β-glucan receptor by Brown & Gordon, *Nature* 2001; the most important β-glucan receptor on macrophages. On binding particulate glucan it signals via a hemITAM → Syk → **CARD9–BCL10–MALT1** → NF-κB/MAPK, driving phagocytosis, respiratory burst/ROS, microbial killing and cytokine production (TNF, IL-6, IL-1β, IL-23 → Th17 support).
- **Complement receptor 3 (CR3, CD11b/CD18)** — predominant for β-glucan modulation of neutrophils, and on NK cells/phagocytes it primes cytotoxicity against iC3b-opsonised targets.
- Lesser/contributing receptors: scavenger receptors, lactosylceramide, and co-operation with TLR2/MyD88 for inflammatory cytokine production to zymosan/live fungi.
— Han et al. 2020 (above, verified live); Brown & Gordon 2001, **index**: https://www.nature.com/articles/35092620?error=cookies_not_supported&code=dc2602f9-512a-4e30-9c1f-bad116ca63e0 ; Brown et al., *J Exp Med* 2003 (Dectin-1 mediates the biological effects of β-glucans), cited in index results.

**Human and mouse genetics corroborate the pathway — this is the strongest evidence tier.** Dectin-1-deficient mice are susceptible to *Candida albicans* and their leukocytes respond poorly to fungi even with opsonins (Taylor et al., *Nat Immunol* 2007, index). In humans, biallelic **CARD9** deficiency (nearly 100 individuals reported) causes life-threatening invasive fungal disease — *Candida*, dermatophytes, phaeohyphomycetes, *Aspergillus* — with impaired cytokine production, neutrophil killing and Th17 responses, while defence against other pathogen classes is largely preserved; human Dectin-1 deficiency gives a milder, mainly mucocutaneous phenotype. A pathway whose loss selectively breaks antifungal immunity is not a supplement-marketing construct.
— CARD9 sources, **index**: https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/190875/version/1/pdf/render.pdf and Drewniak et al., *Blood* 2013 via https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/26965/1/drewniak%20et%20al.%202013%20invasive%20fungal%20infection.pdf

**Trained immunity.** *Candida* infection and fungal β-glucans functionally reprogramme monocytes so they respond more strongly to a later, unrelated challenge. In the founding study this required Dectin-1 and the non-canonical Raf-1 pathway and was associated with stable epigenetic change (H3K4 trimethylation). This is now a defined field (Netea et al., *Nat Rev Immunol* 2020). Caveat for manuscript use: the core demonstration is mouse + human monocytes *in vitro/ex vivo*; a proven clinical benefit of β-glucan “training” in people (e.g. fewer infections because of training, vaccine enhancement) is not yet established.
— Quintin et al., *Cell Host & Microbe* 2012, **index**: https://www.cell.com/cell-host-microbe/fulltext/S1931-3128(12)00232-6

**Other fungal polysaccharides / complexes that belong in the same chapter:**
- **Mannans / mannoproteins** — recognised mainly via Dectin-2/Dectin-3, Mincle and the mannose receptor (α-mannan), signalling likewise through Syk–CARD9.
- **PSK (polysaccharide-K, krestin)** and **PSP** — protein-bound polysaccharides from *Trametes versicolor* (turkey tail); PSK has a TLR2-agonist lipid component as well as its β-glucan fraction.
- **Lentinan** (*Lentinula edodes* / shiitake), **schizophyllan** (*Schizophyllum commune*), **pleuran** (*Pleurotus ostreatus*) — purified β-(1,3)/(1,6)-glucans studied as injectable/oral biological response modifiers in Japan/China.
- **Chitin/chitosan** — size-dependent, often regulatory rather than stimulatory signalling; less clinically developed.

#### Partial / contested — the human clinical evidence

**Oral fungal β-glucans in healthy people and patients: modest, inconsistent, well-tolerated.** The most-cited synthesis, Vlassopoulou et al. 2021, found 34 RCTs (917 records screened) of exclusive oral fungal β-(1,3/1,6)-glucans — sources *Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Aureobasidium pullulans, Pleurotus ostreatus, Lentinula edodes, Ganoderma lucidum* — at **2.5–1,000 mg/day for up to 6.5 months**. Main signal: reduced incidence/symptoms of cold, flu and other respiratory infections, and improved allergic symptoms; **“findings on the induction of immune response alterations were inconsistent at the cellular and molecular levels”**; no adverse event causally related to glucans was recorded; optimal dose and molecular mechanisms still to be established.
— Vlassopoulou et al., *Food Funct* 2021, **verified live**: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33876798/

**Yeast β-glucan and colds specifically.** Zhong et al. 2021 meta-analysed 13 RCTs in healthy subjects and reported significantly reduced upper-respiratory infection incidence (OR 0.345 in secondary summaries), fewer and shorter episodes and lower severity, with the authors noting high heterogeneity and few included studies. Individual large trials are less tidy — e.g. a 299-person trial found lower physical symptom severity in week 1 but no difference in confirmed infection numbers — and many trials are small and industry-funded. Grade this **partial**: a real pooled signal for a narrow outcome (URTI symptoms/incidence with yeast glucan), not a general “immune boosting” proof, and not transferable automatically to mushroom extracts.
— Zhong et al., *Eur J Nutr* 2021, **index** via https://www.nutrasmarts.com/ingredients/wellmune.html and https://www.lifeextension.com/magazine/2025/12/mushrooms-fight-colds-flu

**Cancer adjuncts — the strongest human data, and the most mis-cited.** 
- The US National Cancer Institute PDQ summary states PSK is an approved adjunct in Japan, used in thousands of patients since the mid-1970s, and that a 2007 meta-analysis of **8 RCTs in 8,009 patients** after gastric-cancer surgery suggested patients given chemotherapy + PSK lived longer than chemotherapy alone. It also states plainly: **“The FDA has not approved the use of turkey tail or its active compound PSK as a treatment for cancer or any other medical condition,”** and likewise for reishi.
— NCI PDQ, **verified live**: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/cam/patient/mushrooms-pdq?cid=eb_govdel
- A 2023 systematic review of 39 clinical mushroom studies (2010–2020) found survival benefit reported for PSK in 4 gastric-cancer studies and for Huaier in 3, positive immunological responses in 11 and quality-of-life benefit in 14 — but most studies were small and not RCTs, and **“the evidence is inconclusive to recommend the routine use of mushrooms for cancer patients.”**
— Narayanan et al., *Curr Oncol Rep* 2023, **index**: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369654671_Medicinal_Mushroom_Supplements_in_Cancer_A_Systematic_Review_of_Clinical_Studies
- For colorectal cancer, the 2022 Cochrane review of *Coriolus* preparations pooled 7 trials / 1,569 participants and found only **low-certainty** evidence of a modest 5-year survival benefit, with outdated chemotherapy regimens and unblinded participants (reported in secondary summary, **index**: https://www.elevatesupplements.co.za/blogs/news/understanding-cancer-exploring-natural-support-with-elevate-supplements ).
- A 2026 critical review of mushroom polysaccharides in cancer immunotherapy sharpens the point: receptor evidence is strongest for *general* fungal β-glucan systems (Dectin-1/Syk/CARD9, conditional CR3 co-operation) and cannot be transferred to a named mushroom product without product-specific testing; historical PSK RCTs showed benefit in specific adjuvant settings, a later phase III lentinan trial was **negative for overall survival**, small maitake/*Agaricus*/pleuran studies report mainly immune surrogate markers, and there is **no established direct clinical evidence** with checkpoint inhibitors, therapeutic antibodies or cancer vaccines.
— **Index**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13662765/

**Manuscript-safe phrasing for Q1:** “Fungal β-glucans are bona fide immune ligands with a defined receptor pathway and genetic proof of importance in antifungal defence. Purified preparations have adjunctive clinical history in Japan. For oral supplements in healthy people, trials show good tolerability and a modest, heterogeneous signal on respiratory infections, with inconsistent immune-marker changes — not a demonstrated whole-system enhancement.”

### 2. Mycelial network signaling — electrical/chemical signaling, and honest comparison to neural or immune signaling

#### Established

**Networks transport, and network form follows flow.** Mycelia are branching, fusing (anastomosing) hyphal networks that translocate nutrients, water and cytoplasm over distance and rapidly reorganise when a new resource is found — each hyphal segment is semi-independent, i.e. the system is modular and decentralised. The most rigorous recent demonstration is in arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi: robotic time-lapse imaging tracking >500,000 network nodes and ~100,000 cytoplasmic-flow trajectories showed *Rhizophagus* networks expand as **self-regulating travelling waves** — a pulse of growing tips pulls an expanding wave whose density is self-regulated by hyphal fusion — while maintaining constant transport efficiency to roots, adding loops that shorten paths, and widening/faster-flowing “trunk routes.”
— Oyarte Galvez et al., *Nature* 2025, **verified live**: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41586-025-08614-x
Note its scope: an *in vitro* root-organ-culture AM system, about transport architecture — it demonstrates network-level control of structure and flow, not messages, decisions or cognition.

**Architecture that *could* carry electrical signals.** Filamentous fungi have a continuous plasma membrane, an insulating cell wall (with hydrophobins/melanin — an analogy to, not a homologue of, myelin), and septal pores that can be plugged after damage while membrane continuity persists. Buffi et al. note this makes long-distance electrical propagation physically plausible even when cytoplasmic bulk flow is interrupted.
— Buffi et al. 2025 (below).

**Chemical signalling is established — mainly in yeasts, mainly about density and form.** Quorum sensing in fungi is best characterised in *Candida albicans*: **farnesol** accumulates with cell density and inhibits yeast-to-hypha transition and biofilm formation (via Ras1–cAMP/PKA and other pathways); **tyrosol** does the opposite, accelerating germ-tube formation. In *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, aromatic alcohols (phenylethanol, tryptophol), ammonia and mating pheromones regulate filamentation and collective behaviour. Reports exist in *Cryptococcus, Histoplasma, Aspergillus, Penicillium, Neurospora* and others, but in most filamentous/mycelial species the signal molecules are unidentified, and reviewers caution that “response to exogenous farnesol” is not yet proof of bona fide quorum sensing outside *Candida*.
— **Index**: https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4425/11/4/444/html and https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/24/10/1950/xml

**Electrical activity exists.** From the late 20th century onward, filamentous fungi have been shown to generate action-potential-like signals and electrical currents at hyphal tips, and extracellular potential oscillations/spike trains have been recorded in several mushroom species (below).
— Buffi et al. 2025; Adamatzky 2022.

#### Partial / contested

**Whether electrical spikes *communicate* anything.** Buffi et al., *FEMS Microbiology Reviews* 2025 — the field's current state-of-the-art review — conclude that early work showed AP-like signals and tip currents but “their function in intracellular communication remained unclear” and “the role of electrical signaling as a mechanism of intracellular communication was not irrefutably shown”; the field then stalled and has only recently revived. Studying it is uniquely hard because of microscopic hyphal dimensions, the structural complexity of highly modular networks, and limits of traditional electrophysiology (electrode artefacts, localisation, noise). The review treats plant-to-plant electrical communication via common mycorrhizal networks as a *hypothesis*, and the existence/role of CMNs in interspecies communication as “still highly debated.”
— Buffi et al., *FEMS Microbiol Rev* 2025, **verified live**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11995700/

**Adamatzky's “fungal language.”** Recording four species (ghost *Omphalotus nidiformis*, enoki *Flammulina velutipes*, split gill *Schizophyllum commune*, caterpillar *Cordyceps militaris*) with differential electrodes, Adamatzky found species-specific spikes of **1–21 hours duration, 0.03–2.1 mV amplitude**, clustered into trains. The paper's own abstract makes the load-bearing assumption explicit: *“Assuming that spikes of electrical activity are used by fungi to communicate and process information in mycelium networks, we group spikes into words…”* On that assumption it derives a lexicon of up to ~50 “words” (core 15–20) whose length distribution matches human languages. Adamatzky himself told reporters there may be no direct relationship to human speech, and that spikes could be a by-product (e.g. charged hyphal tips passing an electrode). A published linguistic critique (discussed in Language Log, **index**) argues the patterns show no characteristics of language and may include non-biological noise/artefact. Grade: **the recordings are published and peer-reviewed; the language interpretation is a single-author, assumption-driven analysis, not a field consensus.**
— Adamatzky, *R Soc Open Sci* 2022, **verified live**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8984380/ ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35425630/ ; critique summary, **index**: https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=62109

**Common mycorrhizal networks and the “Wood Wide Web.”** That a single mycorrhizal fungus can colonise two plants, and that isotope-labelled carbon/nutrients can move plant→fungus→plant in experiments (Simard et al., *Nature* 1997, bidirectional birch↔Douglas-fir transfer), is established. The popular extensions are not. Karst, Jones & Hoeksema, *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 2023, examined three claims and found: (1) CMNs are widespread in forests — **insufficiently supported** (network structure genetically mapped in only 5 studies, 2 forest types, 2 tree species, 3 fungal species); (2) resource transfer through CMNs improves seedling performance — **insufficiently supported** (field results variable, alternative explanations, too few studies); (3) mature trees preferentially send resources/defence signals to their offspring through CMNs — **no peer-reviewed published evidence**. They also documented positive citation bias, with unsupported claims in the CMN literature roughly doubling over 25 years. Other researchers dispute the critique (e.g. Klein et al. 2023; Simard et al. response 2025, **index**: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2024.1512518/full ), so grade the *ecological significance* of CMN transfer **contested**, and kin-preference/mother-tree preferential support **unsupported**.
— Karst et al. 2023, **index** (PMID 36782032, DOI 10.1038/s41559-023-01986-1), summarised at https://www.matzen.cloud/article-2026-10-05-the-infrastructure-beneath-the-forest-mycorrhizal-networks-the-wood-wide-web-and-the-limits-of-evidence-en-us.html — note: that summary page is secondary; the claims above are also corroborated in multiple independent index summaries.

**Defence-warning transfer between plants** (e.g. tomato or bean donors connected by AM networks inducing defence enzymes in receivers) rests on a small number of greenhouse/lab studies, with the route (hyphal vs soil diffusion vs root contact) not always excluded. Treat as **partial, lab-bound**.

#### How far can the neural / immune comparison honestly go?

| Dimension | Neurons / nervous system | Immune signalling | Mycelium — what is actually shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signal unit | Action potential, ~1 ms, thresholded, refractory, propagated m/s, released at synapses onto specific targets | Specific ligand–receptor binding (e.g. β-glucan–Dectin-1), cytokine cascades, clonal specificity and memory | AP-*like* spikes lasting **minutes to hours** (Adamatzky: spike duration 1–21 h; intervals ~41–116 min), mV-scale extracellular oscillations; chemical signals are diffusible metabolites/pheromones |
| Specificity / code | Addressed (which neuron → which target) and temporal codes demonstrated by decoding + causal perturbation | Addressed by receptor expression and antigen specificity; loss-of-function genetics prove necessity | No demonstrated code, address, receiver decoding, or causal test that blocking a spike pattern blocks a specific network response |
| Integration | Synaptic integration, centralisation in brains | Distributed, no centre; integration via cell trafficking and cytokine networks | Distributed, no centre; integration demonstrated only as **transport/architecture self-regulation** (Oyarte Galvez 2025), not information integration |
| Legitimate analogy | Shared physics: excitable membranes, ion fluxes | Shared logic: distributed sensing, local response + system-wide state change, “memory” in trained immunity (epigenetic, not synaptic) | Analogy holds at **systems-metaphor level**: decentralised, redundant, plastic networks that sense locally and reallocate resources |
| Where analogy breaks | No neurons, synapses, neurotransmitters, brain, or behaviour driven by spikes has been shown in fungi | Fungi do not have immune cells, antibodies, or antigen-specific memory; quorum molecules are not cytokines | Calling spikes “words,” the network a “brain,” or transfer “care/altruism” adds semantics, intention and function that the experiments did not test |

**Manuscript-safe phrasing for Q2:** “Mycelia are excitable, chemically responsive, self-reorganising transport networks. That licenses comparison to nervous and immune systems as *distributed systems without a controller* — a comparison of organisation, not of mechanism, speed, code or cognition.”

### 3. Encapsulated fungi as immunology models / immune-evasion case studies

#### The central case: *Cryptococcus*

Zaragoza's review opens with why this genus is a model: *Cryptococcus* “offers an excellent model to investigate fungal pathogenesis,” it is a pathogen of concern, and it has evolved pathogenic mechanisms that make it a unique fungal pathogen. Structurally, “*Cryptococcus* spp present a unique phenotypic trait, which is the presence of a capsule that surrounds the cell body,” and a *PLOS Pathogens* review puts it more strongly: *C. neoformans* and *C. gattii* are “differentiated from all other human fungal pathogens by the presence of a gelatinous capsule.” **For a manuscript, do not write “encapsulated fungi” as if it were a large class of human pathogens — in medical mycology it is essentially *Cryptococcus*.**
— Zaragoza, *Virulence* 2019, **verified live**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC6550552/ ; *PLOS Pathog* wall review, **index**: https://Journals.Plos.org/plospathogens/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.ppat.1011141&type=printable

**The two human species complexes:**
- ***C. neoformans* (species complex incl. *C. deneoformans*)** — opportunistic; disease mainly in HIV/immunosuppression (CD4 defect in particular), inhaled from environment → lung → dissemination to brain, cryptococcal meningoencephalitis, historically hundreds of thousands of deaths/year; WHO critical-priority fungal pathogen.
- ***C. gattii* (species complex)** — lower incidence but can cause disease in **immunocompetent** hosts, therefore a primary-pathogen model.
— Zaragoza 2019 (above); Frontiers cryptococcal review, **index**: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2025.1671873/xml

**What the capsule is and does — the immune-recognition lesson:**
- Composition: ~90–95% **glucuronoxylomannan (GXM)** + 5–10% galactoxylomannan/GXMGal + <1% mannoproteins, anchored to the cell wall via α-1,3-glucan; capsule size is dynamically regulated by host-like conditions, and polysaccharide is continuously **shed**.
- Function: anti-phagocytic; **physically masks the underlying PAMPs** (β-glucan, mannans, chitin) so PRRs cannot see them — Dectin-1 is explicitly described as *not indispensable* against *Cryptococcus* precisely because the capsule shields its ligand; sequesters complement/antibody opsonins; shed GXM is immunomodulatory (anti-inflammatory in macrophages, induction of immunological unresponsiveness, Th2 skewing, phagocyte apoptosis in some studies). Acapsular mutants are, with very few exceptions, severely attenuated in mouse infection — the causal test that the capsule is a virulence factor, not a correlate.
— Zaragoza 2019; Frontiers 2025 and *PLOS Pathog* 2023 (above, index); carbon-metabolism/virulence review, **index**: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2022.861405

**Beyond the capsule — why *Cryptococcus* is used to teach the whole evasion repertoire:**
- **Melanin** in the cell wall: antioxidant shield against phagocyte oxidative burst; capsule + melanin are the two classic virulence factors with dual protective/damaging roles (Zaragoza 2019).
- **Facultative intracellular life:** survives and replicates inside macrophages, modulates phagosome maturation, tolerates acid/oxidative stress — traits thought to be pre-selected by environmental predators such as amoebae (the “amoeba as training ground” / accidental-virulence idea, Zaragoza 2019).
- **Vomocytosis (non-lytic exocytosis):** expulsion from the macrophage leaving both cells alive, minimising inflammatory signalling; also proposed as a “Trojan horse” route for dissemination, including toward the brain.
- **Titan cells:** a subset enlarges to 50–100 µm, polyploid, with cross-linked capsule, too large to phagocytose, resistant to oxidative/nitrosative stress, and reducing phagocytosis even of normal-sized neighbours; a mutant with minimal titan production *in vivo* is attenuated, with lower burden and dissemination.
— Titan cells, **index/verified abstract**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3486048/ ; vomocytosis/titan synthesis in Hernández-Chávez et al. 2017 (below).

#### Other fungi used for the same lesson — capsule-*like* and masking strategies, not true capsules

Hernández-Chávez et al., *Journal of Fungi* 2017, catalogue the evasion repertoire across pathogens: cell-wall composition/remodelling and antigen masking, capsule (in *Cryptococcus*), titan cells, biofilms, asteroid bodies, dimorphism, and escape from phagocytosis, complement and extracellular traps, in *Candida, Aspergillus, Cryptococcus, Sporothrix, Paracoccidioides* and others.
— **Index**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC5753153/

- ***Candida albicans* — masking without a capsule.** Its immunogenic β-(1,3)-glucan (~40% of wall mass) is normally covered by an outer mannan/mannoprotein layer. Exposure (“unmasking”) is actively regulated (e.g. by Eng1 glucanase trimming and Ywp1, and by host cues such as lactate and hypoxia); mutants that unmask excessively are recognised better by Dectin-1/CR3/EphA2 and show altered virulence. This is the cleanest demonstration that *recognition is about exposure, not mere presence*, of a PAMP.
— **Index**: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/fungal-biology/articles/10.3389/ffunb.2022.842501/full
- ***Histoplasma capsulatum*** — yeast-phase outer layer of **α-1,3-glucan** hinders detection of underlying β-glucan; also an intracellular macrophage pathogen. (Index, in evasion reviews above.)
- ***Aspergillus fumigatus*** — resting conidia are covered by a hydrophobic rodlet/melanin layer that renders them immunologically relatively inert; on germination the wall remodels and β-glucan/galactomannan become exposed. Galactosaminogalactan also modulates inflammation. (Index, in evasion reviews above.)
- ***Coccidioides* spp.** — spherules grow too large (up to ~120 µm) to be phagocytosed, a size-evasion parallel to titan cells. (Index, 2026 pathogenesis review summary.)
- ***Pneumocystis, Sporothrix, Paracoccidioides, Blastomyces*** — appear in the same evasion literature for wall masking, dimorphism, biofilm/asteroid-body and α-glucan strategies; none is “encapsulated” in the cryptococcal sense.

**What they collectively teach about immune recognition (the transferable principle for a manuscript):**
1. Recognition is **receptor–ligand + accessibility**: the same β-glucan can be a potent alarm or invisible, depending on whether it is exposed, particulate and clustered.
2. Fungi evolve **counter-recognition** (masking, shedding decoy polysaccharide, size change, intracellular refuge) as fast as hosts evolve receptors — an arms race, not a static lock-and-key.
3. Host genetics prove the stakes: loss of Dectin-1/CARD9/Th17 pathways converts commensal/environmental fungi into invasive disease.
4. Protective immunity to *Cryptococcus* is **Th1/CD4- and macrophage-dependent** — which is why cryptococcosis is an AIDS-defining illness, and why immune reconstitution itself can cause pathology (IRIS, in the wider literature).

### 4. Where the published record ends and speculation begins on “fungal networks as a model for whole-body medicine”

#### What the record actually contains, at its outer edge

- **Isolated fungal molecules as conventional drugs/adjuncts** — PSK (Japan), lentinan — i.e. pharmacology of a defined compound at a defined dose, tested (imperfectly) in RCTs. Section 1.
- **The human mycobiome as an association field.** Gut fungi are ~0–1% of the gut microbiota (<0.1% of metagenomic genes), dominated in most cohorts by *Saccharomyces, Malassezia* and *Candida*, with high inter-individual variability and instability, methodological limitations (sequencing/contamination, no standardised diagnostics), and *associations* — not established causation — with IBD, metabolic and other disorders. Reviews state therapeutic strategies (antifungals, probiotics, FMT, diet) “show promise but require further study” / face lack of standardised methodology before clinical integration.
— **Index**: https://www.mdpi.com/2309-608X/11/5/333 and https://www.mdpi.com/2309-608X/11/4/279
- **Mycelium as a material, not a medicine system.** Inactivated *Pleurotus ostreatus* and *Ganoderma lucidum* mycelial mats have been tested *in vitro* as extracellular-matrix-mimicking tissue-engineering scaffolds with preliminary fibroblast biocompatibility — a legitimate biomedical use of network *structure*, with no whole-body therapeutic claim.
— Antinori et al., *Sci Rep* 2021, **index**: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8209158/
- **Network biology as a general scientific style** — decentralised transport networks (fungal, vascular, neural, immune) can be compared mathematically (topology, flow, robustness). Oyarte Galvez et al. 2025 is exemplary *fungal* network science; it makes no medical claim.

#### Where it ends — searches found no peer-reviewed source for:

- Any clinical framework, guideline, trial, or validated model in which a mycelial network is used to diagnose, stratify or treat whole-body human illness.
- Any demonstration that ingested mycelium/fungal networks integrate into, communicate with, or “rewire” human neural, immune, fascial or other networks. Human trials study *extracts and molecules*, absorbed or fermented — not living networks in the body.
- Any evidence for fungal consciousness, intention, language (in the semantic sense), or care/altruism between trees, beyond the contested/unsupported items graded in Section 2.
- The fascia–mycelium parallel that circulates in wellness writing: the only source found for it in this search was a non-peer-reviewed acupuncture/wellness blog explicitly presenting structural resemblance and analogy (“mirrored in nature”), not a journal article.
— **Index**: https://www.toriolsenacupuncture.com/blog/facia-and-mycelium

#### The speculation stack — how the leap is usually built, and where each joint fails

1. *True:* β-glucans talk to immune receptors. → *Leap:* therefore “fungi communicate with our immune system as a network.” **Failure:** a ligand binding a receptor is pharmacology/immunology, not network-to-network communication.
2. *True:* mycelia move resources and show electrical activity in soil/lab. → *Leap:* therefore they are nature's nervous system / brain. **Failure:** no code, synapse, or causal communication function shown (Buffi 2025); timescales differ by orders of magnitude.
3. *True:* mycorrhizal fungi link plants and tracers can move between them. → *Leap:* therefore forests (and by analogy bodies) are cooperative superorganisms that look after their members. **Failure:** Karst et al. 2023 — benefit and kin-preference claims insufficient/unsupported in field evidence.
4. *True:* humans harbour fungi (mycobiome) and fungal molecules affect immunity. → *Leap:* therefore health is a matter of tending an inner mycelial network by analogy to a forest. **Failure:** gut fungi are mostly yeasts, not a demonstrated mycelial network in the human body, and mycobiome causation/therapy is unestablished.

**Bottom line for the manuscript:** “Fungal networks as a model for whole-body medicine” is publishable as **explicit metaphor, design inspiration, or hypothesis** — with the four joints above named as metaphorical. It is not currently publishable as a summary of established medicine, and a manuscript that grades its own claims (established receptor immunology; contested network communication; speculative whole-body analogy) will be *more* credible, not less, for doing so.
