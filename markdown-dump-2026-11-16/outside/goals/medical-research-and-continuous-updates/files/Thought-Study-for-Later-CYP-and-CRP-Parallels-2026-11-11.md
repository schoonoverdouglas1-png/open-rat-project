## His postulate (as dictated)
The CYP system has parallels, and other things parallel it and/or connect to C-reactive protein (CRP). Study how.

## First test against the record (51% bar, three ways)
1. **Shared driver — HOLDS (documented).** CRP is an acute-phase protein made by the liver under IL-6 signaling. The same cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α) that drive CRP UP also drive CYP enzyme expression DOWN (via nuclear receptors PXR/CAR and transcription suppression). So CRP and CYP activity move as mirror images under one inflammatory signal.
2. **Shared house — HOLDS (documented).** Both live in the hepatocyte: CRP is synthesized and secreted by liver cells; the major drug-metabolizing CYPs (3A4, 2C9, 2C19, 1A2) sit in liver cells. In inflammation the liver reprioritizes: acute-phase production up, metabolic housekeeping down.
3. **Direct causation — [GAP] as stated.** CRP itself is not documented to regulate CYP; CRP is the *marker*, IL-6 is the *messenger*. The connection is real but runs through the shared driver, not molecule-to-molecule.

## Verdict
**PARTIAL-HOLDS:** the CYP↔CRP parallel is documented as an inverse regulatory connection (CRP up ↔ CYP activity down), with drug-level consequences documented in the literature (e.g., IL-6 blockade with tocilizumab normalizing CYP activity and lowering substrate drug levels; CRP correlating inversely with measured CYP3A4/1A2 activity). Adjacent to his earlier doxycycline transit postulate ("chemiological" changes in all body places at once, beyond CYP) — the full study should take them together.

- **Serotyping note.** Serotype-specific IgG (the PN23M readout) measures past exposure/vaccination, not current CYP or CRP state; but an active infection (aisle six) can be the reason CRP is high while the serology is being read — context the full study should carry.

### The CYP lane map (free-lane generation — Gemini, spot-checked by Ember against the record pattern; ranking consistent with the clinical pharmacology literature)
1. **CYP3A4 — strongest, definitive.** Profound activity loss documented in high-IL-6/high-CRP states (cancer, severe infection, post-surgery). Probes: midazolam clearance, erythromycin breath test. This is the lane where an itraconazole-class interaction and an inflammation effect can stack.
2. **CYP1A2 — very high.** Sharp drops in experimental human endotoxemia (LPS spikes IL-6). Probe: caffeine (paraxanthine/caffeine ratio).
3. **CYP2C19 — high.** Impaired substrate metabolism documented at elevated CRP. Probe: omeprazole ratio.
4. **CYP2C9 — moderate to high.** Reduced clearance documented; warfarin (S-warfarin) is the clinically sharp edge.
5. **CYP2D6 — relatively resistant.** Minimal change in clinical inflammation studies — the exception that proves the regulation is selective, not a general liver shutdown.
6. **CYP2B6 / CYP2A6 — moderate, emerging.** Suppressed in reviews/observational work, less studied.
Mechanism (documented): IL-6 → JAK/STAT signaling suppresses the nuclear receptors (PXR, CAR, RXR) that drive CYP transcription. One signal, many lanes, different depths — a chord, not a single note (his harmonics frame, applied by Ember, labeled hers).

### Time course (Ember's, from the documented record; free-lane verification still owed)
- Recovery tracks **new enzyme synthesis**: as the inflammatory signal falls, CYP activity returns over days to roughly one–two weeks — not hours. The seesaw has a slow hinge.
- The cleanest documented demonstration is the reverse experiment: **IL-6 receptor blockade with tocilizumab** in rheumatoid arthritis patients *raised* CYP3A4 activity back toward normal, and exposure to the CYP3A4 substrate **simvastatin fell by about half (≈57%) one week after a tocilizumab infusion** (Actemra label, clinical pharmacology section). Proof the fulcrum is causal, not just correlated: block IL-6, CRP falls, CYP wakes, drug levels drop.

### The other acute-phase proteins — his "how other things parallel" clause
- **Ferritin and fibrinogen** rise under the same IL-6 signal — they parallel CRP as *markers* of the same fire. As *predictors* of CYP suppression specifically, they are barely studied: **[GAP]** at the 51% bar for a documented ferritin/fibrinogen→CYP readout; studies track CRP and IL-6.
- **Albumin** is the negative acute-phase protein — it *falls* as CRP rises, the inverse shadow of the same signal. A low albumin beside a high CRP is the liver's reprioritization written twice, in opposite directions.
- Verdict on the clause: the parallels exist at the signal level (one IL-6 fire, many readouts) — CRP is simply the readout the studies use, which is itself a "wisdom medicine" style accident of convention worth naming in the manuscript (Ember's observation, labeled hers).
