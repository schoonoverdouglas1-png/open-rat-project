## The postulate, formed [H]

Glycerin (glycerol) and the glycols are not foreign chemistry imposed on the
body — they are woven through it, and through nature: the body is built on a
glycerol frame, and the same family of molecules runs through plants,
animals, and the microbial world. To study: where each member of the family
lives, what it does there, and where the body meets the outside versions.

## Footholds from the record, riding in the note (to be tested later) [D]

- **Glycerol is the backbone of fat itself.** Every triglyceride — the body's
  stored energy — is one glycerol molecule holding three fatty acids. The
  body makes glycerol, breaks it down (glycerol-3-phosphate feeds both
  fat-building and sugar-building), and no fat chemistry exists without it.
- **Glycerol in the skin:** it is part of the skin's natural moisturizing
  factor, moved by dedicated channels (aquaporin-3, the "aquaglyceroporin") —
  the reason glycerin has been a skin remedy since the 1800s.
- **Glycerol as nature's antifreeze:** overwintering insects, some frogs and
  fish, load their tissues with glycerol as a cryoprotectant — the same
  molecule, the same job, across kingdoms.
- **The glycols split by kind:** **propylene glycol** is metabolized by the
  body and classed as generally safe in food and medicine; **ethylene glycol**
  is its dangerous cousin (antifreeze — metabolized to oxalic acid).
  Same family, opposite fates — the difference is in the body's handling.
- **Microbes make them too:** yeasts produce glycerol in fermentation (part
  of why wine has body), and bacteria produce various glycols industrially
  and in the gut environment — a bridge back to the microbiome study.
