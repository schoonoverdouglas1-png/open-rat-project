### 1c. Test two — do azoles act on viruses at all? A narrow, named foothold, not a class effect [D for the foothold; GAP for arboviruses]

- Documented azole mechanism, fungal track: azoles inhibit fungal CYP51 (lanosterol 14α-demethylase), blocking ergosterol synthesis and thereby fungal growth (Strating et al. summary of itraconazole's antifungal action; PubMed 14742211 on CYP51 as the azole target). Viruses have no ergosterol and no CYP51 of their own, so the class mechanism does not transfer to viruses by itself.
- The real foothold: Strating et al., *Cell Reports* 10:600–615 (2015), "Itraconazole Inhibits Enterovirus Replication by Targeting the Oxysterol-Binding Protein" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124714011218) — itraconazole, found by screening an FDA-approved drug library, inhibited enteroviruses (poliovirus, coxsackievirus, enterovirus-71, rhinovirus) and hepatitis C virus in vitro, **not** through CYP51/CYP3A4 but by binding the host oxysterol-binding protein OSBP/ORP4 and blocking its cholesterol/PI4P exchange at membrane contact sites, which those viruses need to build replication organelles. In the same screen, posaconazole also inhibited replication; fluconazole, voriconazole, and ketoconazole did **not** display antiviral activity. So even the foothold is drug-specific (itraconazole, partly posaconazole), not azole-class-wide.
- A cautionary second data point: a proof-of-concept randomized trial of itraconazole for COVID-19 (EBioMedicine 2021; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7979145/) found in-vitro activity against SARS-CoV-2 but concluded itraconazole is "unlikely to be of clinical benefit" for that virus — in-vitro signal did not become clinical effect there.
- **The [GAP]:** no source found in this pass tests any azole against any of the four ARBOP viruses (La Crosse / California, Eastern equine, St. Louis, Western equine). Those are a bunyavirus (La Crosse) and alphaviruses / a flavivirus (EEE, WEE, SLE) — none is an enterovirus, and none is documented to depend on OSBP the way enteroviruses and HCV were shown to. At Douglas's 51% bar: "itraconazole can inhibit some non-arboviruses in vitro via a host target" is documented [D]; "azoles as a class clear arboviruses" breaks as stated (wrong mechanism, class members inactive in the one screen, no arbovirus data); the arbovirus-specific application stays [GAP], not upgraded and not discarded — the exact handling his pneumonia rule requires for partials.
