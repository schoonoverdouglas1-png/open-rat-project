### Way 1 — "Part of the DNA itself": holds, documented [D]

- Human endogenous retroviruses (HERVs) are viral sequences integrated into the genome. Stein & DePaola, *Physiological Genomics* 2023 (PubMed 37154499): "Approximately 8% of the human genome, over four times more than its protein-coding regions, comprises sequences of viral origin that are known as human endogenous retroviral elements (HERVs)." They "resulted from the integration of now-extinct exogenous retroviruses into mammalian ancestor germ cells or their precursors," are present in all human cells, are mostly silenced by mutation and epigenetic change, and are vertically transmitted.
- The germ cell is the bridge between his "eggs" wording and the DNA claim: a retrovirus that integrated into a germ cell — the lineage that produces eggs and sperm — is thereafter inherited in a Mendelian way, like any other gene. That is the documented mechanism behind "part of the DNA itself," not a metaphor.
- Some integrated viral material was later co-opted for host function: the same review names syncytin-1 and syncytin-2, HERV-encoded proteins critical to placenta formation in embryogenesis. So a minority of viral DNA is not only present but working for the host.
- Honest limit inside this way: most HERVs are defective remnants — silenced, mutated, not infectious viruses sitting whole inside the DNA. "Viral sequence in the DNA" holds; "live virus in the DNA, ready to go" does not, as a general statement.

### Way 2 — Seeds: holds strongly for plants [D]

- Pagán, *PLoS Pathogens* 2022, "Transmission through seeds: The unknown life of plant viruses": parent-to-offspring vertical transmission through seeds "has been known for a century"; the number of seed-transmitted plant viruses increased six-fold from the ~100 described 35 years earlier, "with some reaching 100% of infected seeds"; vertically transmitted persistent (cryptic) viruses are predominant in wild plants and common in crops, and for those viruses seed transmission is the only way to infect new hosts. Routes are direct invasion of the embryo and infection of ovules and/or pollen leading to an infected embryo after fertilisation.
- Escalante et al., *Frontiers in Plant Science* 2024: "Approximately one-third of plant viruses are seed-transmitted," vertical transmission occurs directly through the embryo or indirectly through pollen grains or ovules, and reported seed-transmission rates in economically important crops range from near zero to over 90% for some virus–crop pairs.
- Crucial distinction for his filing: seed-carried plant viruses are usually carried as virus in the embryo, seed coat, or reproductive tissues — they are **passed through the seed without necessarily being integrated into the plant's DNA**. The seed half of his postulate therefore holds on the transmission track, by a different mechanism than the DNA half.

### Way 3 — Eggs / germline in animals, happening now: holds for a specific, observable case [D], and the universal form breaks

- Koala retrovirus (KoRV-A) is the live counterexample to "this only happened in the deep past": it is a retrovirus still in transition from exogenous (animal-to-animal) to endogenous (integrated into germline cells and passed parent-to-offspring as part of the genetic code). The search record for this pass summarised that transition and its germline insertions across wild koalas; the underlying claim — exogenous retrovirus infecting germ-line cells becomes heritable — is the same mechanism the HERV review documents for human ancestors.
- Counter-evidence / scope check, run as the third angle against the universal wording:
  - **Not all viruses:** integration into host DNA is a retrovirus life-cycle feature (and a narrower set of other endogenous viral elements exists); most viruses — including most seed-transmitted plant viruses above — do not integrate at all.
  - **Not all alive things:** eggs and seeds are the reproductive propagules of animals and plants respectively. The claim has no equivalent foothold as stated for organisms that reproduce without eggs or seeds, and no source opened in this pass documents viral integration or egg/seed carriage as a universal of all life.
  - **Not "within" every egg or seed:** seed transmission is virus- and host-specific, rates vary from 0% to 100%, and presence in a seed does not always mean transmission to the seedling (Pagán 2022 makes that detection-vs-transmission distinction explicitly).

### Verdict

**[PARTIAL] hold at the 51% bar, in narrowed form.** Restated so it fits the record: *Some viruses are part of the DNA itself — retroviruses that integrated into germ cells, now inherited as endogenous viral elements, about 8% of the human genome — and many other viruses are carried into the next generation inside seeds (about a third of known plant viruses) and, in the animal germline case, through egg/sperm lineages, and are passed around that way.* The universal form — viruses within the eggs and seeds of **all** alive things — **breaks** as stated: it overreaches the documented taxa, mechanisms, and virus families.

Simply put (6th–8th grade parallel, per his dual-register standing for chapters): Some old viruses really did get written into our DNA long ago, when they infected the cells that make eggs and sperm — about 8 out of every 100 pieces of human DNA came from viruses that way. And in plants, many viruses really do ride inside seeds into the next generation. But not every virus does this, and not every living thing has eggs or seeds, so "all alive things" is too big. The smaller version is solid.

Support implication: when Douglas returns to this thread, lead with the narrowed restatement and the two different mechanisms side by side — (1) integrated into DNA (retrovirus → germ cell → inherited) vs. (2) carried in a seed/embryo (no integration required) — because collapsing those two is the one error this postulate invites. It connects cleanly to his already-filed "Encounters with the Biome" postulate (2026-11-11: an antibody panel as an encounter log written by carriage as well as disease): endogenous viral elements are the deepest version of an encounter log — encounters written into the genome itself.
