## Classification distinctions captured in the indexed grouping key

An indexed excerpt from `Serotype_and_Disease_Grouping_Key_FINAL.xlsx` distinguishes several grouping systems:

- Lancefield group: streptococcal cell-wall group carbohydrate; not capsular serotype.
- Capsular serotype: capsular polysaccharide or protein antigenic type; not necessarily a lineage or virulence class.
- Capsular serogroup: broader, scheme-specific capsule grouping; not interchangeable with an exact serotype.
- O/H serovar: somatic O and flagellar H antigens; not a clonal lineage or pathotype.
- Protein serotype/serosubtype: defined surface-protein epitopes; not capsule serogroup.
- *emm*/M-protein type: sequence variation in the *emm* gene/M protein; not Lancefield group.
- Biotype, pathotype, toxigenicity, viral serotype, HA/NA subtype, genotype/sequence type, lineage/clonal complex, and diagnostic antigen biomarker are distinct concepts and require their own evidence criteria.

## Multi-model audit excerpt

The available indexed excerpt from the 2026-11-04 multi-model assimilation identifies several evidence gaps and corrections:

- Quantitative cross-reactivity percentages were not established for every serogroup member.
- A single definitive disease ranking across pneumonia, meningitis, otitis media, and invasive disease was not established by the reviewed material.
- Full direct-comparison lists for *Cryptococcus* and GBS remained incomplete or partial.
- The Duck.ai model selector was reported to differ from older model guides; the excerpt records the selector observed on 2026-11-04 and notes that future lists should be checked against the live selector.
- Web Search availability differed by model in that run; the excerpt notes that one model had Web Search disabled, which affected the comparability of its responses.

The same excerpt distinguishes molecular lineage methods (such as MLST or clonal complexes) from antigenic classification based on capsule chemistry. Capsule switching is a reason not to equate lineage with capsule type. It also cautions that vaccine inclusion or structural similarity does not, by itself, guarantee a measured immune response or clinical protection.
