## Clarification of Two Meanings of ALS
agglutinin-like sequence (ALS) gene family = Candida albicans (C. albicans) fungal adhesin family - Als1-Als7 and Als9 - cell-surface glycoproteins that have adhesive function - yeast velcro for biofilm
amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) = Lou Gehrig's disease - human neurodegenerative disease of motor neurons - human genes superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1), chromosome 9 open reading frame 72 (C9orf72) hexanucleotide repeat expansion GGGGCC, transactive response DNA-binding protein 43 (TARDBP), fused in sarcoma (FUS)

## Subsection Three: Is There Any Research Linking Candida Yeast With Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)?
### Short Answer
No established causal link. There is a small hypothesis literature proposing fungal infection including Candida in central nervous system (CNS) tissue of some amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) patients, but it is investigational, not accepted as cause, not in treatment guidelines. Fungal agglutinin-like sequence (ALS) genes do NOT cause human amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) disease.
### Research Found - Grouped by Evidence Status
Evidence status definitions per your Evidence Handling:

Established = licensed and well documented, Investigational = in trials or peer-reviewed but not consensus, Experimental = preclinical or early research, Verify = AI-generated or secondary needs primary check, Hypothesis = proposed mechanism not proven, Gap = no data
#### 1. Direct Tissue Studies - Investigational / Hypothesis
Study: Alonso et al., Neurobiology of Disease Dec 2017 - Fungal infection in neural tissue of patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)

Immunohistochemistry analysis using a battery of antifungal antibodies revealed fungal structures such as yeast and hyphae in the motor cortex, the medulla and the spinal cord, in eleven patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)
Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis and next generation sequencing of DNA extracted from frozen neural tissue identified a variety of fungal genera including Candida, Malassezia, Fusarium, Botrytis, Trichoderma and Cryptococcus
Finding: Candida albicans (C. albicans) antibody recognized yeast-like structures in ALS central nervous system (CNS) tissue
Limitation: n=11, no controls for contamination, correlation not causation, other groups have not replicated with same methods, fungi are ubiquitous environmental contaminants
Status: Investigational / Hypothesis - not causal proof
Source: PubMed PMID 28847377, Neurobiol Dis 2017

Study: Pisa, Alonso et al., Frontiers in Microbiology 2015-2020 - Searching for Bacteria in Neural Tissue From Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)

We recently advanced the idea that ALS might be caused by fungal infection. Indeed, fungal yeast and hyphal structures can be directly visualized in neural tissue of ALS patients, and a number of fungal species have been identified in the central nervous system (CNS)
Our results indicate that bacterial DNA and prokaryotic cells are present in CNS tissue, leading to the concept that both fungal and bacterial infections coexist in patients with ALS
Also used rabbit polyclonal antibody against Candida albicans (C. albicans)
Status: Hypothesis - same group proposing fungal origin
#### 2. Environmental / Epidemiological Hypothesis - Hypothesis / Gap
Study: French Alps amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) hot spot associated with genotoxic fungi

Food use of False Morel mushrooms (Gyromitra gigas) in ALS cases but not controls
ALS linked to fungal hydrazinic toxins with acute toxicity and long-term DNA lesions
All patients had ingested wild mushrooms, notably poisonous False Morels. Half reported acute illness following Gyromitra gigas consumption
This supports hypothesis that genotoxins of fungal origin may induce long-term motor neuron degeneration
Status: Hypothesis, small cluster, not Candida albicans (C. albicans) or Candida auris (C. auris) specific, genotoxic mushroom toxins not agglutinin-like sequence (ALS) genes

Study: Fungal-contaminated grass and well water and sporadic amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)

We review several lines of evidence that point to a potential fungal origin of sporadic amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), the most common form of motor neurone disease. We have previously postulated that grass or soil-associated fungal infections may be a leading cause of sporadic ALS
Proposed mechanism: opportunistic fungal infection from environmental exposure to water, soil, plants leads to long-term neuronal degradation
Status: Hypothesis review, not primary data linking Candida yeast specifically
#### 3. Gut Dysbiosis and Candida albicans (C. albicans) in ALS patients - Investigational
Study: Gut inflammation and dysbiosis in human motor neuron disease (Rowin et al., 2017)

Patient with definite amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) reported with candida albicans 2+ on culture
Culture: candida albicans 2+ | Dysbiosis by culture
Finding: Some ALS patients show gut dysbiosis including Candida albicans (C. albicans) overgrowth
Interpretation: Secondary to dysbiosis, antibiotics, dysphagia, immune changes — not cause of ALS
Status: Investigational / Association not causation
#### 4. No Link Between Fungal agglutinin-like sequence (ALS) Gene Family and Human amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) Genes - Gap / Complete Error = Fantasy One Time
Human amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) major pathogenic genes: superoxide dismutase 1 (SOD1) ~12-20% familial, chromosome 9 open reading frame 72 (C9orf72) GGGGCC hexanucleotide repeat expansion ~40% familial, transactive response DNA-binding protein 43 (TARDBP), fused in sarcoma (FUS) - responsible for just over two thirds of familial forms and >5% sporadic
Two decades after discovery that 20% of familial amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) cases were linked to mutations in superoxide dismutase-1 (SOD1) gene, substantial proportion of remainder traced to expansion of intronic hexanucleotide repeat sequence in C9orf72
No sequence homology between Candida albicans agglutinin-like sequence (ALS) adhesins (Als1-Als9) and human SOD1, C9orf72, TARDBP, FUS proteins
Complete errors = fantasy one time: Claiming Candida albicans agglutinin-like sequence (ALS) genes cause human amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) because same abbreviation ALS would be complete error = fantasy
