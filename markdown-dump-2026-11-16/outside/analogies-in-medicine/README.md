# Analogies in Medicine

A collected shelf of the analogies medicine actually runs on — gathered, labeled, and tested.

**Commissioned:** Nov 11, 2026, by Douglas Roy Schoonover, from the waiting room at Eau Claire, minutes after the founding pair below was spoken aloud.
**Built by:** Douglas Roy Schoonover and Purple Rat ("Ember"), per his standing rule that search projects start as GitHub projects.

## The postulate (his idea, formed as a theory per his method)
Medicine is taught, remembered, and reasoned with through analogies — the army, the pump, the lock and key, the cookbook. Collect them all in one place, label where each one comes from, and test where each one breaks. An analogy that hides its breaking point misleads; an analogy with its breaking point labeled teaches twice.

## Method
Every entry carries:
- **The analogy** as it is actually used.
- **What it maps** — the mechanism it stands for.
- **Where it breaks** — the point past which it misleads (his classroom rule: a "breaks" verdict is the class working, not a failure).
- **Provenance** — [D] documented in general use · [H] his · [E] Ember's · [GAP] origin not yet verified. Quick-draft entries are seeded from general knowledge and marked for the three-way check in the full sweep.

## The founding pair (Nov 11, 2026 — [E], spoken in the WW2 nurse-in-training telling)
1. **Neutrophils are the first soldiers to a trouble spot** — they arrive first and eat bacteria.
2. **Lymphocytes are the intelligence corps** — they remember old enemies and make the antibodies.
3. Beside them, the **hundred-cell count**: a manual differential counts 100 white cells, so the shares must add to 100 — when one kind's share of the hundred rises, another's falls, even if the army itself hasn't changed size. Look at the *number* of each kind, not only its share.
