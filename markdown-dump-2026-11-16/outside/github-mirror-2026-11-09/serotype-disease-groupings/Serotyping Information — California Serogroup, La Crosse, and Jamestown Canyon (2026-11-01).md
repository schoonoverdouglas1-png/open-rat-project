## The correction that has to come first

There is **no separate "La Crosse serogroup" and no separate "Jamestown Canyon serogroup"** in the record. There is **one** serogroup — the **California serogroup (CSG)** — and La Crosse virus and Jamestown Canyon virus are two **members** inside it, alongside California encephalitis virus, snowshoe hare virus, trivittatus virus, and the rest.

That is why the three entries below are kept separate but nested: the serogroup first, then each virus on its own values. Collapsing them into three parallel serogroups would invent a taxonomy that does not exist. Keeping them separate as *members* is the honest version of his request.

## 1. The California serogroup (CSG) — the whole group

- **Taxonomy:** order Bunyavirales, family Peribunyaviridae, genus *Orthobunyavirus*.
- **What a "serogroup" means here:** a group of viruses sorted by **antibody cross-reactivity** — the antibodies raised against one member react, to varying degrees, with the others. "Serogroup classification of viruses has traditionally been a way to group similar viruses based on the cross-reactivity of the antibodies they elicit." This is **not** bacterial serotyping: there is no capsule chemistry here. The sorting key is serology plus genetics.
- **Genome and size (shared by the group):** trisegmented, negative-sense RNA viruses; virions about **90–100 nm** (CDC Peribunyaviridae band), enveloped, with surface glycoprotein spikes.
- **Prototype member:** California encephalitis virus (CEV), "first isolated from mosquitoes in California in 1943".
- **Member count — variance carried, not smoothed over:**
- 2019 review: "Currently, there are 18 recognized members of the CSG based on serological and genetic evidence. In addition to CEV, these are La Crosse virus (LACV), Snowshoe hare virus (SSHV), Tahyna virus (TAHV), Jamestown Canyon virus (JCV), Inkoo virus (INKV), Chatanga virus (CHATV), Keystone virus (KEYV), Jerry Slough virus (JSV), Lumbo virus (LUMV), Melao virus (MELV), San Angelo virus (SAV), Serra do Navio virus (SDNV), South River virus (SORV), Trivittatus virus (TVTV), Morro Bay virus (MBV), Achiote virus (ACHOV), and Infirmatus virus (INFV)."
- Wikipedia's California encephalitis page: "The California serogroup of viruses includes seventeen viruses" — the 17-vs-18 gap is a counting / inclusion difference in the sources (Guaroa virus "was briefly categorized as a CSG virus, however follow-up studies have since indicated it is a distinct virus").
- U.S. surveillance short list (the six that matter for U.S. testing): California encephalitis, Jamestown Canyon, Keystone, La Crosse, snowshoe hare, trivittatus.
- **Human disease in the group:** "Of these viruses, at least seven have been shown to cause neurological disease in humans, including the leading cause of pediatric arboviral encephalitis in the USA, La Crosse virus." The five neuroinvasive members most discussed are LACV, SSHV, TAHV, JCV, and INKV.
- **Vectors and hosts (group level):** "All members of the CSG are presumed arboviruses, as all have been isolated from mosquitoes". Mammalian amplifying hosts vary by member (rodents, hares, deer — see the member entries).
- **Why serotyping is hard in this group:** "Diagnosis of specific CSG viruses is complicated by the high degree of antibody cross-reactivity between them, with laboratory standards requiring a fourfold higher titer of neutralizating antibody (NAb) activity to positively identify the etiologic virus." And the honest limit: "a fourfold difference in NAb titer between the inoculum virus and the other CSG viruses was not always observed. Thus, NAb titers, which are the gold-standard for diagnosing the etiologic agent for viral encephalitis, may not clearly differentiate between different CSG viruses."
- **Typing method that does separate members:** an antibody-capture ELISA procedure "to type California serogroup viruses" distinguished "Seven California serogroup members from North America were distinguished, including snowshoe hare, La Crosse, California encephalitis, San Angelo, Jamestown Canyon, Keystone, and trivittatus. Extensive cross-reactions were observed between Jamestown Canyon and the closely related South River strain."

### The other North American members, briefly (so LACV and JCV are not mistaken for the whole group)
- **California encephalitis virus (CEV):** prototype, 1943; "only 4 cases of CEV encephalitis were reported in humans during 1943–2001".
- **Snowshoe hare virus (SSHV):** "originally identified in Montana in 1961 and has been associated with several neurologic disease cases in children in North America".
- **Trivittatus virus (TVTV):** "originally isolated from *Aedes trivittatus* mosquitoes in North Dakota in 1952 and has been associated with at least 1 case of viral encephalitis".
- **Keystone virus (KEYV), San Angelo virus (SAV):** North American members, typed by the ELISA above; KEYV is on the U.S. surveillance list.
- **Tahyna (TAHV), Inkoo (INKV):** chiefly Eurasian neuroinvasive members; TAHV "most often manifests as influenza-like symptoms and only rarely leads to encephalitis".

## 2. La Crosse virus (LACV) — separate values

- **Place in the group:** member of the California serogroup, genus *Orthobunyavirus*. **Not** a serogroup of its own.
- **Disease name:** La Crosse encephalitis / La Crosse virus disease. "LACV is the leading cause of pediatric viral encephalitis in the United States, responsible for ≈50–100 reported cases per year."
- **Age pattern:** primarily children. "LACV, SSHV, TAHV, and INKV primarily cause neuroinvasive disease in children", with LACV the most pathogenic of the group.
- **Vector:** eastern tree-hole mosquito *Aedes triseriatus* (primary); *Aedes canadensis* also reported. Amplifying hosts: chipmunks and squirrels.
- **Geography:** Upper Midwest and Appalachian / eastern U.S.; the virus is named for La Crosse, Wisconsin, where the first fatal pediatric case was recognized.
- **Seroprevalence in endemic regions:** approximately 1–27% (LACV, JCV, and SSHV grouped in that band).
- **Diagnosis — serology first:**
- "Serologic testing remains the primary method for diagnosing La Crosse virus disease. La Crosse virus is difficult to isolate from clinical samples, and almost all isolates and viral RNA have come from brain tissue or rarely cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)."
- "a preliminary diagnosis of La Crosse virus disease can be made by detection of La Crosse virus-specific IgM antibodies in serum or CSF."
- Confirmation rule: "Because of potential cross-reactivity in the IgM assay to related viruses (e.g., Jamestown Canyon virus), a positive La Crosse virus IgM test result should be confirmed by neutralizing antibody testing of serum or CSF specimens at a state public health laboratory or CDC."
- Molecular tests (RT-PCR) are early-course only and "might be more sensitive than serologic testing" for immunocompromised patients.
- **Cross-reactivity profile:** "Antigenically, LACV is closely related to viruses such as Jamestown Canyon virus and Snowshoe hare virus making the development of antigen-antibody tests that do not cross-react with other California serogroup viruses challenging." In the mouse cross-reactivity study, "LACV, SSHV, and INKV elicited the highest amount of cross-reactive NAb".
- **Arbopanel note (his record):** his September 2025 arbopanel carries a single "California (La Crosse)" analyte — California encephalitis virus and La Crosse virus are **not** separated on that panel. That panel has no separate Jamestown Canyon analyte. This file does not interpret his panel; it only states the panel structure on file.

## 3. Jamestown Canyon virus (JCV) — separate values

- **Place in the group:** member of the California serogroup, genus *Orthobunyavirus*. **Not** a serogroup of its own.
- **Disease name:** Jamestown Canyon virus disease / Jamestown Canyon encephalitis.
- **First isolation:** "first isolated in 1961" near Jamestown, Colorado.
- **Age pattern — the mirror image of LACV:** "JCV appears to preferentially cause neuroinvasive disease in adults", while LACV hits children. "several cases occur every year in the United States and Canada", and "the number of reported cases increased substantially in the 2010s, JCV is considered a potentially emerging arboviral disease".
- **Vector and host:** multiple *Aedes* (and *Culiseta*) mosquitoes; primary amplifying host thought to be white-tailed deer. Wisconsin and Minnesota account for more than half of recognized U.S. cases (on file from Oct 16, 2026 verification).
- **Case counts — three figures, three scopes, all carried:**
- 31 disease cases in the U.S. from 2000 to 2013 (older CDC summary).
- "Between 2011-2022 there were 281 confirmed JCV infections in the USA that occurred across 23 states".
- Literature review 1982–2022: "A total of 50 reports with 416 cases of JCV were found. The primary illness associated with JCV infection involved the nervous system. Of reported encephalitis cases, the fatality rate was 2.4 percent in hospitalized patients."
- The rise is attributed to testing, not necessarily to a new virus: "Reports of acute Jamestown Canyon Virus (JCV) cases have increased markedly over the last 15 years, associated with improved diagnostic testing protocols."
- **Asymptomatic load:** "several studies have found a high prevalence of seropositivity for Jamestown Canyon virus in humans, suggesting a high proportion of asymptomatic infections."
- **Diagnosis — serology first, with a JCV-specific trap:**
- "Serologic testing remains the primary method for diagnosing Jamestown Canyon virus disease."
- "a preliminary diagnosis of Jamestown Canyon virus disease can be made by detection of Jamestown Canyon virus-specific IgM antibodies in serum or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)."
- "A positive Jamestown Canyon virus or California serogroup virus IgM test result should be confirmed by neutralizing antibody testing at a state public health laboratory or CDC."
- "it is not known how long IgM antibodies can be detected in serum following a Jamestown Canyon virus infection; therefore, a positive IgM antibody test may reflect past infection."
- Most accurate recent-infection call: "diagnosis of a recent Jamestown Canyon virus infection is most accurately made by testing acute and convalescent sera to assess for at least a 4-fold rise in neutralizing antibody titers."
- CDC confirmation wording: "JCV or LACV IgM antibodies in serum with JCV-specific neutralizing antibodies equal or more than 4-fold higher than LACV-specific neutralizing antibody titers in the same specimen or a later specimen".
- History of the test: "The CDC has used plaque reduction neutralization tests to detect JCV neutralizing antibodies since 1995. The test is automatically done on all samples testing positive or equivocal for La Crosse Virus IgM antibodies by ELISA. In 2010 CDC developed an ELISA also for JCV IgM."
- Recent seroprevalence method: screening by "90% plaque reduction neutralization test at 1:10 dilution", with "a ≥4-fold higher titer against 1 virus than the other used to determine the more likely infection" between JCV and LACV. In that study, "Two specimens had indistinguishable neutralizing antibody titers between Jamestown Canyon and La Crosse viruses" — the 4-fold rule sometimes cannot separate them.
- **Misdiagnosis note:** "JCV cases have also been misdiagnosed as another member of the CSG, La Crosse virus (LACV), due to cross-reactivity of NAb responses". In Wisconsin 2011–2016, of 30 JCV case patients, "20 (67%) had test results that showed cross-reactivity, false-positive IgM results that did not confirm with PRNT, or a previous infection (i.e., IgG antibodies) with another arbovirus (LACV or WNV)".

## What "serotyping" does and does not mean here

- **Does:** it groups these viruses by shared antibody reactions (serogroup), then tries to name the single member by the highest neutralizing titer, using the 4-fold rule.
- **Does not:** give each virus a capsule type the way pneumococcus gets serotypes 1, 3, 23F. There is no capsule, and no 100-type list. The California serogroup's "types" are the 17–18 member viruses themselves.
- **The honest limit, kept in the file:** even the gold-standard PRNT "may not clearly differentiate between different CSG viruses" in some samples. A single IgM result, without PRNT, does not name the member.
