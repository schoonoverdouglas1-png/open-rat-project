## Summary

**Vaccine lists (DOCUMENTED, §1–§2).** The seven named vaccines are not a simple ladder. PCV7 → PCV10 (GSK) → PCV13 → PCV15 → PCV20 *is* a strict superset chain. PCV21 **breaks** the chain: it drops nine PCV20 serotypes and adds eleven disease serotypes, for a different adult-targeted set. PPSV23 is unconjugated and is neither a superset nor a subset of any PCV. And “PCV10” is **not one vaccine**: GSK Synflorix and Serum Institute Pneumosil differ in four serotypes (§2, §6.4).

**Union across all seven vaccines:** 32 distinct disease serotypes (counting 15C as a disease serotype reached by PCV21's modified 15B antigen, and counting serogroup-20 as one “20/20A” entry — see §6.2–§6.3): 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15A, 15B, 15C, 16F, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20/20A, 22F, 23A, 23B, 23F, 24F, 31, 33F, 35B.

**Serogroups (PARTIALLY DOCUMENTED at best, §4).** The Danish/Quellung serogroup is an *antigenic* grouping (serotypes sharing capsular-polysaccharide reactivity), not a phylogenetic or *cps*-locus cluster — the two systems disagree in places (§4.4). For vaccine-relevant groups, membership is confirmed for most groups by a second source, but Sunfire's 2022 table is demonstrably incomplete after 2020 (missing 15D, 20C, 24C, 33E/G/H, 36A/B, 10D, and serogroup 48) and conflicts with the SSI latex-pool list on 11E. Single-serotype (in the Sunfire/SSI sense) vaccine-relevant groups are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 14 and 31 — with the caveat in §4.2 that “single” is serological, not genetic (e.g. serotype 14's *cps* locus is related to serogroup 15).

**Latin disease names (GAP for per-serotype names, §5).** No retrieved label, review or classification assigns a *different* Latin disease name to each serotype, and a targeted search for Latin disease phrases (“pneumonia pneumococcica”, “meningitis pneumococcica”) returned no such nomenclature. What *is* documented is: (a) the organism's Latin binomial, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*; (b) vaccine-level disease terms — which are themselves Latin/Greek-derived medical terms (*pneumonia, meningitis, sepsis, bacteraemia/bacteremia, otitis media, sinusitis, empyema, peritonitis*, etc.) — differing by vaccine and by age group, with two hard restrictions (PCV13 otitis media only for the seven PCV7 serotypes; PCV21 pneumonia excluding 15B). Applying a vaccine's disease list uniformly to every serotype in that vaccine is an inference, not a documented per-serotype claim, and is flagged as such wherever it would matter. **Do not publish a per-serotype Latin-disease table from this research; it would be invented.**

**Bacterial/fungal relationships (DOCUMENTED only in narrow form, §5.4).** The documented bacterial relationship is within the *Streptococcus* Mitis group: *S. mitis, S. oralis* and *S. infantis* carry *cps* loci highly homologous/antigenically related to specific pneumococcal serotypes — documented in detail for serotype 5, and by PCR homology for 12F, 15A, 18C and 33F, plus a second paper's claim for 2, 4, 9A, 13 and 23A (PARTIALLY DOCUMENTED, not opened in full). There is **no** documented mapping of a pneumococcal serogroup onto a fungal serogroup. *Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii* serotypes A, B, C, D (and AD) are a separate, glucuronoxylomannan-based system; the only retrieved pneumococcus–fungus link is an unnamed “some antigenic determinants shared with certain pneumococcal polysaccharides” — GAP for any per-serotype fungal relationship. Any table equating, e.g., pneumococcal serogroup 15 with a fungal serotype would be invented.

### How the lists relate to each other

- **Strict chain:** PCV7 ⊂ PCV10-GSK ⊂ PCV13 ⊂ PCV15 ⊂ PCV20. Each step adds only: PCV10-GSK +1, +5, +7F; PCV13 +3, +6A, +19A; PCV15 +22F, +33F; PCV20 +8, +10A, +11A, +12F, +15B. This chain is DOCUMENTED by the CDC/PMC review (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11869777/) and by each label.
- **PCV21 breaks the chain.** Shared with PCV20 (polysaccharide level): 3, 6A, 7F, 8, 10A, 11A, 12F, 19A, 22F, 33F (10), plus the modified 15B antigen. Dropped vs PCV20: 1, 4, 5, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, 23F (9). Added vs PCV20: 9N, 15A, 16F, 17F, 20A, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B (10 polysaccharides) + 15C by cross-reactivity (disease level). The “8 unique serotypes” figure (15A, 15C, 16F, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B) means *unique vs any other currently licensed vaccine including PPSV23* — it is **not** the count added vs PCV20 (11 disease serotypes), because 9N, 17F and 20A are in PPSV23. Conflating those two counts is a common error; see §6.3.
- **PPSV23 vs PCV20:** PPSV23 adds 2, 9N, 17F, 20/20A and lacks 6A (the only PCV20 serotype absent from PPSV23). PPSV23 vs PCV21: PPSV23 adds 1, 2, 4, 5, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, 23F (and 15B in unmodified form) and lacks all eight PCV21-unique serotypes plus 15A, 16F, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B in the sense above — i.e. PCV21's disease list minus the PPSV23 overlap (3, 6A, 7F, 8, 9N, 10A, 11A, 12F, 15B, 17F, 19A, 20A, 22F, 33F) = 15A, 15C, 16F, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B.
- **PCV10-SII vs PCV13:** Pneumosil is PCV13 minus 3, 4, 18C. That is, it is *not* a subset of Synflorix (it adds 6A, 19A) and *not* a superset of it (it drops 4, 18C).

## 4. Serogroup-grouped list

### 4.1 What a “serogroup” is — and what it is not

DOCUMENTED (MDPI cross-reactivity review, 2024, https://www.mdpi.com/2076-393X/12/9/974): “The distinct chemical composition and immunological properties of the capsular polysaccharide define pneumococcal serotypes; antigenically related serotypes are categorized into serogroups.” The Danish system (Statens Serum Institut, SSI) groups serotypes by cross-reaction with typing antisera (Quellung/Neufeld reaction); the trailing letter distinguishes members (e.g. serogroup 19 = 19F first, then 19A, 19B, 19C — Sheffield Laboratory Medicine summary, https://sheffieldlaboratorymedicine.nhs.uk/search-test.php?search=3380).

It is **not** the same as:
- a *cps*-locus genetic cluster (Mavroidi et al. 2007: all serotypes in a serogroup fall in the same *major* cluster, but in six cases serotypes in one serogroup are in *different* subclusters, and nine subclusters contain serotypes from different serogroups; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2168730/);
- a vaccine group (PCV7/PCV13/… groupings are by inclusion, not antigenic relatedness);
- a bacterial species group (the Mitis group, §5.4) or a fungal serotype system (§5.4).

### 4.4 Why serogroup ≠ genetic cluster — a discriminating example

Mavroidi et al. (2007) clustered 88 *cps* loci into eight major clusters and 21 subclusters: all members of one serogroup fell in one major cluster, but six serogroups split across subclusters and nine subclusters mixed serogroups. A later computational analysis grouped 90 serotypes into 64 “serogroups” by glycosyltransferase gene — a *different* grouping from Quellung serogroups (e.g. it merges 40/7F/7A, 38/25F/25A, 37/33F/33A, which Quellung separates). **For Douglas's books/papers, the grouping system must be named every time**: “Danish/Quellung serogroup”, “*cps*-locus cluster”, or “PCR genogroup” — otherwise two tables that both say “group” will contradict each other without either being wrong.

## 5. Latin disease names, and bacterial/fungal relationships

### 5.1 What “Latin” can and cannot mean here

The organism's name **is** Latin (binomial nomenclature): *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (Klein 1884) Chester 1901, formerly *Diplococcus pneumoniae* (1920–1974, renamed because of similarity to streptococci). The carrier organism for CRM197 is likewise Latin: *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*; the Synflorix protein-D source is *Haemophilus influenzae* (non-typeable); tetanus toxoid derives from *Clostridium tetani*. Those Latin binomials are DOCUMENTED and are the only per-organism Latin names this research can attach to the vaccines.

Disease names in the labels are **not** given in Latin phrases. They are modern medical terms of Latin/Greek derivation, used in English in every label retrieved: *pneumonia, meningitis, sepsis/septicaemia, bacteraemia/bacteremia, otitis media, sinusitis, empyema, peritonitis, pericarditis, endocarditis, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, cellulitis, bronchitis, rhinitis, conjunctivitis, brain abscess*. A targeted deeper search for Latin disease phrases (§6.7) found **no** “pneumonia pneumococcica / meningitis pneumococcica” nomenclature in retrieved sources. Presenting such phrases as documented Latin disease names would be invention. The honest rendering for Douglas's framework is therefore: **Latin binomial for the organism + Latin-derived disease terms in the form the labels actually use**, with the per-vaccine differences in §5.2, and an explicit GAP for per-serotype Latin names in §5.3.

### 5.3 Per-serotype Latin disease names — GAP, stated plainly

**No source retrieved assigns a distinct set of Latin (or Latin-derived) disease names to serotype 3 vs serotype 8 vs serotype 19A, etc.** Surveillance papers associate serotypes with *frequencies* of invasive disease, meningitis or case-fatality (e.g. South African data associating certain serotypes with higher IPD case-fatality), but those are epidemiological associations, not disease-name assignments, and they vary by age group and country. A table with one row per serotype and a column “Latin disease name(s)” filled for every row would therefore be **fabricated** from this evidence base. The correct table for Douglas's use is: one row per *vaccine* (§5.2), plus the statement that within a vaccine, the label does not differentiate disease terms by serotype except for the two hard restrictions above (PCV13 otitis media; PCV21 pneumonia/15B). If Douglas's framework *requires* a per-serotype disease column, that column must be left blank/marked GAP and re-sourced from serotype-specific clinical studies — it cannot be filled from vaccine labels.

### 5.4 Serotype groupings and their bacterial and fungal relationships

**Names of the grouping/relationship systems — use these exact names, do not blur them:**
1. **Danish/Quellung serogroup** (§4) — antigenic, SSI antisera.
2. ***cps*-locus cluster / genogroup** — genetic, Mavroidi/PneumoCaT/SeroBA.
3. **Mitis group (viridans streptococci)** — species-level phylogenetic group containing *S. pneumoniae* and its close relatives.
4. **Vaccine group** (PCV7/13/15/20/21/PPSV23 types) — inclusion, not biology.
5. **Fungal serotype system** (*Cryptococcus*) — GXM-based, unrelated to 1–4 except for the unnamed shared-determinant note below.

**Bacterial relationships — DOCUMENTED, serotype by serotype (do not generalise):**

**General Mitis-group relationship (DOCUMENTED):** *S. pneumoniae* and *S. mitis* are “thought to have evolved from a common precursor” (2025 Mitis encapsulation paper, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12473126/); the Mitis group, within viridans streptococci, includes about 20 species, and its “Pneumoniae subclade” groups *S. pneumoniae, S. pseudopneumoniae, S. mitis, S. oralis, S. infantis, S. peroris* and *S. timonensis* (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7669753/). Most *S. mitis/oralis/infantis* strains examined carry full-length *cps* operons, some highly similar to pneumococcal counterparts, but only a minority react with pneumococcal typing sera — so “carries a similar *cps* locus” ≠ “expresses a pneumococcal serotype”.

**Fungal relationships — what is documented and what is not:**

- *Cryptococcus neoformans* (fungus, yeast) and *Cryptococcus gattii* are encapsulated fungi whose capsules are primarily **glucuronoxylomannan (GXM)** (+ galactoxylomannan), not pneumococcal-type oligosaccharide repeat units. Their serotypes are **A, D and AD** (*neoformans/grubii*) and **B and C** (*gattii*), defined by rabbit-serum reactivity with GXM (https://clinicalpub.com/cryptococcosis-cryptococcus-neoformans-and-cryptococcus-gattii/; https://www.antiinfectivemeds.com/articles/cryptococcus-neoformans/). **There is no mapping A↔pneumococcal group, B↔group, etc., in any retrieved source, and none should be created.**
- The single retrieved bridge is a chapter summary stating the cryptococcal capsule “has some functional similarities to those of encapsulated bacteria such as *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Haemophilus influenzae*” and “is known to share some antigenic determinants with **certain** pneumococcal polysaccharides” (Zaragoza et al. chapter, via https://www.academia.edu/29950718/Chapter_4_The_Capsule_of_the_Fungal_Pathogen_Cryptococcus_neoformans). **The pneumococcal serotype(s) involved are not named in the retrieved text.** Status: **GAP for any per-serotype fungal relationship.** The functional similarity (capsule as antiphagocytic virulence factor, capsule-based serotyping) is a *conceptual* parallel across kingdoms, not a serogroup relationship.
- Other encapsulated bacteria that share the *concept* of capsule-based serogrouping (for Douglas's framework, as parallels, not as relatives): *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroups A, B, C, W135, X, Y (of 12–13 capsular serogroups); *Haemophilus influenzae* serotypes a–f (encapsulated strains); *Escherichia coli* K1 capsular antigen; Group B *Streptococcus* (*S. agalactiae*) serotypes. These are documented as parallel classification systems (Mayo meningitis/encephalitis panel description), **not** as pneumococcal serogroup relatives. Do not merge their letter systems (meningococcal “B” ≠ pneumococcal “6B” ≠ cryptococcal “B”).
