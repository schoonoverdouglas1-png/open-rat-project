## Fungal inoculation lifts milk thistle yield and silymarin
In semiarid Egyptian field plots, white-flowered milk thistle inoculated with arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi yielded about 27% more seed than uninoculated controls, according to [the field study](https://bioengineer.org/underground-fungal-allies-supercharge-milk-thistle-yields-and-healing-compounds/). The purple-flowered variety paid out differently, with roughly 36% more silymarin in its seeds, the flavonolignan complex behind the crop's liver-protective reputation.
